## Interview Preparation Checklist: Your Night-Before Action Plan

Interview preparation doesn't require weeks of study. This interview preparation checklist gives you everything to review, pack, and practice the night before—so you walk in confident and ready.

## Night Before Checklist

Print this checklist or copy it to your notes app:

### Research Review (10 minutes)

- [ ] Re-read the job description—highlight 3 key requirements
- [ ] Review company "About" page and mission statement
- [ ] Check recent news (Google: "[Company Name] news")
- [ ] Look up your interviewer(s) on LinkedIn
- [ ] Note 1 company fact to mention: recent launch, award, or initiative

### Story Preparation (15 minutes)

- [ ] Prepare 2 STAR stories that match job requirements
- [ ] Write bullet points for each story (not full scripts)
- [ ] Practice saying each story in under 2 minutes
- [ ] Prepare a 60-second "Tell me about yourself" answer
- [ ] Think through your "Why this company?" answer

### Logistics (5 minutes)

- [ ] Confirm interview time and time zone
- [ ] Save interviewer contact info (in case you're late)
- [ ] Plan your route or set up video call link
- [ ] Set 2 alarms: one for wake-up, one for departure/login
- [ ] Lay out interview outfit

### Video Interview Extra Steps

- [ ] Test camera, mic, and speakers
- [ ] Check internet stability
- [ ] Set up clean, professional background
- [ ] Position lighting in front of you (not behind)
- [ ] Close unnecessary browser tabs and apps

## Quick STAR Story Template

The STAR method structures your answers so they're clear and memorable. Use this template to prepare 2-3 stories.

### STAR Framework

| Letter | Meaning | What to Include |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **S** | Situation | Context in 1-2 sentences |
| **T** | Task | Your specific responsibility |
| **A** | Action | What YOU did (not the team) |
| **R** | Result | Quantified outcome + what you learned |

### Fill-In Template

> **Situation:** At [Company], we faced [challenge/situation].
>
> **Task:** My responsibility was to [your specific role].
>
> **Action:** I [specific actions YOU took—use "I" not "we"].
>
> **Result:** This led to [quantified result]. I learned [key takeaway].

### Example STAR Story

**Question:** Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem.

> **Situation:** At my previous company, our customer churn rate spiked 25% over two months, and leadership didn't know why.
>
> **Task:** As the Customer Success lead, I was asked to identify the root cause and propose solutions within two weeks.
>
> **Action:** I analyzed support tickets for patterns, interviewed 15 churned customers, and surveyed 200 active users. I discovered our new onboarding flow was confusing power users who needed advanced features.
>
> **Result:** I redesigned the onboarding with branching paths based on user goals. Within 3 months, churn dropped 18% and our NPS score increased 12 points. I learned that assumptions about users often don't match reality—direct conversation reveals the truth.

### Story Topics to Prepare

Have STAR stories ready for these common themes:

- [ ] A time you solved a difficult problem
- [ ] A time you led or influenced others
- [ ] A time you failed and what you learned
- [ ] A time you worked under pressure/deadline
- [ ] A time you disagreed with a coworker or manager

## 5 Questions to Ask Your Interviewer

Asking thoughtful questions shows genuine interest. Choose 2-3 from this list:

### About the Role

1. "What does success look like in this role in the first 90 days?"
2. "What's the biggest challenge someone in this role would face?"
3. "How would you describe the team's working style?"

### About Growth

4. "What opportunities for growth or learning does the company offer?"
5. "Where do you see this role or team in a year?"

### About the Interviewer

6. "What do you enjoy most about working here?"
7. "What's something you wish you'd known before joining?"

**Avoid:** Questions easily answered by the website, salary/benefits (save for later rounds), or "When will I hear back?" (ask HR, not the interviewer).

## "Tell Me About Yourself" Template

This question opens most interviews. Use the Present-Past-Future formula:

### Formula

1. **Present:** What you do now (1 sentence)
2. **Past:** Relevant experience that led here (2-3 sentences)
3. **Future:** Why this role excites you (1-2 sentences)

### Example

> I'm currently a Marketing Manager at HubSpot, where I lead our content strategy for the SMB segment. I started my career in journalism, which taught me how to tell compelling stories, then transitioned to tech marketing at a startup where I built our blog from zero to 50,000 monthly readers. I'm excited about this role at Spotify because I want to apply my content expertise to a product I personally love and use every day.

**Length:** 60-90 seconds maximum. Practice with a timer.

## Morning-Of Checklist

### 2 Hours Before

- [ ] Eat a light meal (avoid heavy food that causes drowsiness)
- [ ] Review your STAR story bullet points (don't cram new info)
- [ ] Do a quick tech check for video interviews
- [ ] Get dressed in your interview outfit

### 30 Minutes Before (Video)

- [ ] Close all unnecessary apps and tabs
- [ ] Put phone on silent (and out of reach)
- [ ] Have water nearby
- [ ] Open the video link 5 minutes early
- [ ] Take 3 deep breaths

### 30 Minutes Before (In-Person)

- [ ] Arrive at location (wait nearby, don't enter yet)
- [ ] Use restroom, check appearance
- [ ] Turn phone to silent
- [ ] Bring copies of resume, notepad, and pen
- [ ] Enter building 10-15 minutes before interview time

## What to Bring (In-Person)

- [ ] 3-5 copies of your resume
- [ ] Notepad and pen
- [ ] List of questions to ask
- [ ] Portfolio or work samples (if relevant)
- [ ] Government ID (some offices require sign-in)
- [ ] Breath mints (use before entering, not during)
- [ ] Water bottle (optional)

## Quick Calm-Down Techniques

Nervous? Use these 60-second techniques:

### Box Breathing

1. Inhale for 4 seconds
2. Hold for 4 seconds
3. Exhale for 4 seconds
4. Hold for 4 seconds
5. Repeat 3-4 times

### Power Pose

Stand tall, hands on hips, chin up for 2 minutes before the interview. Research shows this reduces cortisol and increases confidence (even if you do it in a restroom stall).

### Reframe Nerves

Instead of "I'm nervous," tell yourself "I'm excited." The physical sensations are identical—your brain believes the label you give it.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Better Approach |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Arriving too early | Inconveniences team, seems anxious | Arrive at location early, enter 10-15 min before |
| Not asking questions | Signals low interest | Prepare 3-5 thoughtful questions |
| Badmouthing former employer | Major red flag | Focus on what you learned, keep it positive |
| Rambling answers | Loses interviewer attention | Use STAR structure, aim for 90-120 seconds |
| Not following up | Misses chance to reinforce interest | Send thank-you email within 24 hours |
