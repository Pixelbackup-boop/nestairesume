## Formatting & Structure Tips

For a Thai graphic designer, the cover letter is itself a design sample, so let layout do half the talking. Use a tasteful sans-serif (Helvetica, Inter, or a clean Thai font such as Sukhumvit or Noto Sans Thai if you write any Thai), keep generous white space, and consider a thin accent rule or a small monochrome logotype of your initials in the header. Studios in Bangkok and remote agencies notice kerning, alignment, and type hierarchy instantly — a sloppy letter undercuts every claim about your eye for detail. Put your portfolio URL in the very first line of the header (next to your name and LINE/email), because the creative director clicks Behance, Dribbble, or your personal site before reading paragraph two.

In the body, open by naming the studio's recent work — a rebrand, a packaging line, a campaign you genuinely admire — and tie it to your strength in brand identity, UI/UX in Figma, or bilingual Thai/English typography. Frame results the way a design lead thinks: "redesigned the LINE OA assets and lifted sticker-set CTR 28%," "delivered 40+ social templates per month in Adobe Creative Suite," or "built a Figma component library that cut handoff revisions in half." Keep it to one page, name the exact tools from the job post (Photoshop, Illustrator, After Effects, Figma), and mention whether you can localize layouts for Thai script — that bilingual nuance is a real differentiator most applicants skip.

## ATS Optimization

Many employers use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to screen cover letters before a human reads them. To pass these filters, mirror keywords from the job description naturally throughout your letter. Avoid using headers in text boxes, tables, or graphics that ATS software cannot parse. Save your file as a PDF unless the employer specifically requests a different format.

Include the exact job title and key qualifications mentioned in the posting. If the role requires "project management experience," use that exact phrase rather than a synonym. This ensures your application scores well in automated screening while still reading naturally to hiring managers.
