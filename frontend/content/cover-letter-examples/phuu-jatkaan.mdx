## Formatting & Structure Tips

For a Thai manager (phuu-jatkaan / ผู้จัดการ) role, the executive or HR director reading your letter is screening for scope and P&L ownership, so lead the opening paragraph with the size of what you've run: team headcount, departments or branches overseen, and annual budget or revenue responsibility in baht. State your functional area plainly — operations, sales, branch, project, or general management — and whether you've managed in a Thai-owned, multinational (MNC), or family-business context, because the leadership expectations differ sharply between them. Keep it to one page in a professional font; if the employer is an MNC or you're targeting a regional role, providing both a Thai and an English version signals the bilingual reach these positions require.

Frame your body paragraphs around the results a hiring committee in Thailand actually measures: revenue or branch growth (%), cost reduction, KPI/OKR attainment, headcount you developed or promoted, and any turnaround or expansion you led. Reference the management tools and frameworks the posting names — Balanced Scorecard, SAP or Oracle ERP, Six Sigma, or a specific CRM — and show cross-cultural and stakeholder skill if the company works across ASEAN or with overseas head offices. A line such as "led a 45-person operations team across three provinces, lifting on-time delivery from 82% to 96% while cutting logistics cost 14%" demonstrates command better than "strong leadership skills." Mirror the seniority and language of the job ad so the letter reads as a peer, not a generalist.

## ATS Optimization

Many employers use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to screen cover letters before a human reads them. To pass these filters, mirror keywords from the job description naturally throughout your letter. Avoid using headers in text boxes, tables, or graphics that ATS software cannot parse. Save your file as a PDF unless the employer specifically requests a different format.

Include the exact job title and key qualifications mentioned in the posting. If the role requires "project management experience," use that exact phrase rather than a synonym. This ensures your application scores well in automated screening while still reading naturally to hiring managers.
