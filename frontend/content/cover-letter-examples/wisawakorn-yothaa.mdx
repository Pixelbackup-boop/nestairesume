## Formatting & Structure Tips

For a Thai civil engineer cover letter, put your Council of Engineers Thailand (COE) licence number and grade — ภาคีวิศวกร (Associate), สามัญวิศวกร (Professional), or วุฒิวิศวกร (Senior) — directly under your name in the header. Consulting firms, contractors, and government agencies treat the COE licence as a pass/fail gate, so reviewers scan the top inch of the page for it before reading a single sentence. Keep the letter to one page, use an engineering-clean font like Arial or Times New Roman at 11pt, and align it with the bilingual (Thai/English) convention many Bangkok EPC and BOI-promoted firms expect.

Build your two body paragraphs around discipline and deliverables, not adjectives. Open the first with the structures you have stamped or supervised — high-rise reinforced concrete, post-tensioned slabs, bridge piers, or precast foundations — and tie each to a quantified outcome: cubic metres of concrete poured, span lengths, percentage under budget, or zero lost-time incidents on site. Reference the codes you design to (TIS standards, EIT guidelines, or ACI/Eurocode where the project demands) and the tools you actually use (AutoCAD, STAAD.Pro, Tekla, or Revit), since hiring engineers verify that your software claims match the firm's BIM workflow. Close by naming the project type the employer is hiring for — infrastructure, commercial, or industrial — so the letter reads as a fit for that pipeline rather than a generic engineering form letter.

## ATS Optimization

Many employers use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to screen cover letters before a human reads them. To pass these filters, mirror keywords from the job description naturally throughout your letter. Avoid using headers in text boxes, tables, or graphics that ATS software cannot parse. Save your file as a PDF unless the employer specifically requests a different format.

Include the exact job title and key qualifications mentioned in the posting. If the role requires "project management experience," use that exact phrase rather than a synonym. This ensures your application scores well in automated screening while still reading naturally to hiring managers.
