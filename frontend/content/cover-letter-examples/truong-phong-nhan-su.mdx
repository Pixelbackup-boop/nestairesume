## Formatting & Structure Tips

You are applying to lead the very function that screens cover letters, so yours has to be exemplary — the CEO or COO reading it expects the document you would coach your own team to write. Keep it to one polished page with an executive-summary feel: a confident opening that states the scope you lead (headcount supported, sites, budget), a body organized around your strongest HR outcomes, and a close that frames you as a strategic partner to the leadership team, not just a policy administrator. Use a clean, formal font and faultless grammar, because as the HR Department Head you set the bar for professional communication across the company.

Lead with metrics that prove business impact, not activity: turnover or attrition reduced (e.g., "cut voluntary turnover from 22% to 13% in 18 months"), time-to-fill and offer-acceptance rates, engagement or eNPS gains, and cost savings in benefits or recruiting spend. Signal command of the full function and its compliance stakes — talent acquisition, performance management, compensation and benefits design, employee relations and investigations, and labor-law adherence (FLSA, FMLA, EEO, ADA, or the relevant local labor code). Name the systems you have run (Workday, SuccessFactors, Bamboo HR, ADP, Greenhouse) and any HRBP, SHRM-SCP, SPHR, or CIPD credentials, since these are common ATS filters for HR leadership roles. Throughout, show you can translate people strategy into board-level outcomes — that altitude is what separates a department head from a generalist.

## ATS Optimization

Many employers use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to screen cover letters before a human reads them. To pass these filters, mirror keywords from the job description naturally throughout your letter. Avoid using headers in text boxes, tables, or graphics that ATS software cannot parse. Save your file as a PDF unless the employer specifically requests a different format.

Include the exact job title and key qualifications mentioned in the posting. If the role requires "project management experience," use that exact phrase rather than a synonym. This ensures your application scores well in automated screening while still reading naturally to hiring managers.
