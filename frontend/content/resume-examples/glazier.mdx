## What Makes a Great Glazier Resume?

Glaziers install glass in buildings—from skyscraper curtain walls to residential windows. The work requires precision, as glass is unforgiving of mistakes. With +5% job growth and an average salary of $48,000, the Glazier field offers solid career opportunities for skilled tradespeople. Your resume must communicate your certifications, hands-on experience, and ability to work safely and efficiently. This guide covers what employers look for when hiring Glazier professionals.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Glazier:**
"Motivated Glazier with hands-on training in Glass Installation and Curtain Wall. Completed apprenticeship/training program with strong foundation in Storefront. Eager to apply skills in a professional environment with commitment to safety and quality work."

**For Experienced Glazier:**
"Skilled Glazier with 5+ years of experience in Glass Installation and Curtain Wall. Proven track record of completing projects on time while maintaining excellent safety record. Experienced in Storefront with strong troubleshooting abilities."

**For Senior Glazier:**
"Master Glazier with 15+ years of experience leading crews and managing complex projects. Expert in Glass Installation, Curtain Wall, and Storefront. Trained 20+ apprentices and maintained zero-incident safety record over past 5 years."

## Glazier Resume Format & Template Tips

Trade resumes need to communicate competence quickly. Hiring managers often review dozens of applications—make yours stand out:

- **Certifications at the top** — License numbers, certification types, and expiration dates should be immediately visible. This is the first thing employers verify
- **Quantify your experience** — Years in trade, number of projects, production metrics, and safety record with specific numbers
- **Specify equipment and systems** — List specific brands, models, or systems you're experienced with. Generic descriptions don't differentiate you
- **Include safety record** — Incident-free years, safety training completed, and OSHA certifications demonstrate professionalism
- **Show progression** — Apprentice to journeyman to master, crew lead to foreman—show career growth
- **One page preferred** — Trades value efficiency. Keep it concise and relevant

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not specifying commercial vs residential

Curtain wall and residential work require different skills. Be clear about your experience

### Missing project scale

Square footage of curtain wall, number of units installed—metrics demonstrate experience

### Ignoring system experience

Kawneer, YKK, Oldcastle—mention specific systems you're experienced with

### No safety certifications

Include OSHA, aerial lift, and fall protection training. Glass work involves heights

### Not mentioning specialty glass experience

Fire-rated, impact-resistant, decorative—specialty glass experience adds value

## Common Glazier Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Glazier interviews, along with guidance on how to answer them:

### "What glazing systems are you most experienced with?"

Discuss curtain wall, storefront, residential, and specific manufacturer systems.

### "How do you ensure proper installation and weatherproofing?"

Cover setting blocks, sealant application, and testing procedures.

### "Describe your experience working at height"

Discuss swing stage, scaffolding, and aerial lift experience. Include certifications.

### "How do you handle damaged or broken glass?"

Cover safety procedures, proper disposal, and replacement processes.

### "What specialty glass have you worked with?"

Discuss fire-rated, laminated, low-e, or other specialty products.
