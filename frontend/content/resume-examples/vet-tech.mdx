## What Makes a Great Vet Tech Resume?

This vet tech resume example shows exactly what hiring managers want to see. The right resume format makes the difference between landing an interview and getting filtered out. Veterinary technicians are the backbone of animal healthcare, performing clinical procedures, assisting in surgery, and educating pet owners. Your resume should highlight your clinical skills, the types and volume of cases you have handled, and your credentials (CVT, RVT, or LVT). Specialization in areas like dentistry, emergency care, or exotic animals can distinguish you from other candidates.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"Credentialed Veterinary Technician (CVT) and recent AVMA-accredited program graduate with 500+ clinical hours across general practice, emergency, and surgery rotations. Proficient in venipuncture, radiology, anesthesia monitoring, and laboratory diagnostics. Passed the VTNE on the first attempt. Passionate about companion animal care."

**For Mid-Level:**
"Registered Veterinary Technician (RVT) with 4 years of clinical experience in a busy small-animal practice seeing 40+ patients daily. Skilled in anesthesia induction and monitoring, dental prophylaxis, and in-house lab diagnostics. Trained 6 veterinary assistants and maintained a 98% client satisfaction rating. Fear Free Level 2 certified."

**For Senior:**
"Licensed Veterinary Technician (LVT) with 9+ years of experience across general practice, emergency medicine, and specialty surgery. Lead technician managing a team of 8 in a multi-doctor practice. VTS (Emergency and Critical Care) certified with expertise in ventilator management, blood transfusions, and advanced pain management protocols."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

### Clinical Procedures
- Venipuncture and IV catheter placement
- Anesthesia induction, monitoring, and recovery
- Dental scaling, polishing, and radiology
- Surgical preparation and sterile technique
- Radiology positioning and digital X-ray
- Wound care and bandage application

### Laboratory & Diagnostics
- In-house lab diagnostics (CBC, chemistry, urinalysis)
- Microscopy (cytology, fecal analysis, skin scraping)
- Blood gas analysis and point-of-care testing
- Sample collection and external lab submission
- Ear cytology and dermatological diagnostics
- Heartworm, FeLV/FIV, and parvovirus testing

### Patient Care & Communication
- Patient triage and assessment
- Medication calculation and administration
- Client education on treatment plans and medications
- Fear Free and low-stress handling techniques
- Medical record documentation (Cornerstone, Avimark)
- Controlled substance logging and inventory management

## Achievement-Focused Bullet Points

- "Performed venipuncture, IV catheter placement, and lab sample collection on an average of 25 patients per day with a 95% first-attempt success rate"
- "Monitored anesthesia for 1,200+ surgical procedures over 3 years including spays, neuters, dental cleanings, and orthopedic surgeries with zero anesthetic complications"
- "Trained and mentored 6 new veterinary assistants on clinical procedures, restraint techniques, and patient monitoring, reducing their ramp-up time by 3 weeks"
- "Managed in-house laboratory operations, running 30+ diagnostic panels daily and maintaining quality control records that passed all AAHA inspection requirements"
- "Implemented Fear Free handling protocols across the clinic, reducing patient stress scores by 40% and increasing client satisfaction survey results by 15%"
- "Maintained 100% accuracy in controlled substance logging across 2,000+ dispensing events over a 4-year period"

## Vet Tech Resume Format & Template Tips

Choosing the right resume format is critical for animal care roles:
- **Chronological resume format** — Best for vet tech candidates with steady career progression through clinical and specialized roles
- **Professional resume format** — Use clean section headers, consistent spacing, and a modern but readable font
- **ATS-friendly resume template** — Avoid graphics, tables, and columns that confuse applicant tracking systems
- **Resume length** — 1 page for entry-level / 1-2 pages for experienced / 2 pages for senior
- Save your resume as PDF to preserve your resume format across all devices and ATS platforms

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Not listing your credential prominently** — CVT, RVT, or LVT should appear in your header or professional summary; VTNE pass status matters for new graduates
2. **Being vague about clinical skills** — Specify the procedures you perform independently versus those you assist with
3. **Ignoring patient volume** — Hiring managers want to know the pace of your previous workplace; include daily patient counts
4. **Omitting specialty experience** — Emergency, dental, exotic, or surgical specialization is a significant differentiator
5. **Forgetting software proficiency** — Veterinary practice management systems like Cornerstone, Avimark, or eVetPractice should be listed
