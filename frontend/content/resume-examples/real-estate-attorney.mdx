## What Makes a Great Real Estate Attorney Resume?

A real estate attorney resume must demonstrate transactional expertise across property types and deal sizes. Hiring partners evaluate candidates based on deal sheet depth, transaction complexity, and client relationship management ability.

The most effective real estate attorney resumes lead with representative transactions (without client names unless permitted) and highlight specific deal values, property types, and closing complexity managed.

## Professional Summary Examples

### Entry-Level Real Estate Attorney
Junior associate with 2 years supporting commercial real estate transactions at Am Law 200 firm. Experience in due diligence, title review, and closing coordination for acquisitions, financings, and leases. Strong research and drafting skills with exposure to $50M+ deals.

### Mid-Level Real Estate Attorney
Real estate associate with 5 years handling commercial transactions including acquisitions, dispositions, financings, and joint ventures. Closed 75+ transactions totaling $800M+ in aggregate value. Expert in title examination, survey review, and complex title insurance negotiations.

### Senior Real Estate Attorney
Partner-track real estate attorney with 8+ years and $2B+ in closed transactions. Lead attorney on major portfolio acquisitions, sale-leasebacks, and development projects. Manage associate workload and client relationships. Known for creative problem-solving on complex title and zoning issues.

Strong real estate attorney bullet points quantify transaction experience:

- Closed 85+ commercial transactions totaling $1.2B in aggregate value

## Real Estate Attorney Resume Format & Template Tips

1. **Lead with a deal sheet summary** listing representative transactions by type and value
2. **Include bar admissions** prominently—jurisdiction matters for where you can practice
3. **Specify property types** handled (office, retail, multifamily, industrial, hospitality)
4. **Highlight transaction complexity** not just dollar amounts
5. **Mention client types** (developers, REITs, lenders, institutional investors) if permitted

## Hiring Manager Tip

> **"I look at the deal sheet first. I want to see variety of transaction types, increasing complexity over time, and deals where you were the primary attorney—not just supporting. Dollar amounts matter less than what you actually did on the deal."**

Hiring partners evaluate real estate attorneys on transaction breadth and depth. Your resume should demonstrate progression from due diligence support to lead attorney roles. Include property types, transaction structures, and your specific role on each deal. Client names add credibility when permitted.

## Common Real Estate Attorney Interview Questions

### 1. Walk me through your role on a recent significant transaction
Describe the deal structure, your responsibilities, challenges you navigated, and the outcome. Be specific about what YOU did versus the team.

### 2. How do you approach title examination for a complex commercial property?
Explain your systematic review process, common issues you look for, and how you resolve curative matters.

### 3. Describe a deal where something unexpected arose and how you handled it
Show problem-solving ability with a specific example—title issues, survey problems, environmental findings.

### 4. How do you manage multiple closings with competing deadlines?
Discuss organization, delegation, communication with clients, and prioritization strategies.

### 5. What property types or transaction structures interest you most?
Show genuine interest and align your preferences with the firm's practice focus.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **No deal sheet or transaction list**—this is expected for real estate attorneys
2. **Generic litigation or corporate experience** without real estate specifics
3. **Omitting bar admissions and jurisdictions** where you can practice
4. **Dollar amounts without role clarity**—explain what you personally handled
5. **Missing property types** showing your transaction breadth
