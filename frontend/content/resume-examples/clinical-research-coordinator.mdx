## What Makes a Great Clinical Research Coordinator Resume?

This clinical research coordinator resume example shows exactly what hiring managers want to see. The right resume format makes the difference between landing an interview and getting filtered out. A strong CRC resume demonstrates your ability to manage the day-to-day operations of clinical trials, from patient screening and enrollment to data collection and regulatory compliance. Principal investigators and study sponsors value coordinators who keep trials on schedule while maintaining impeccable documentation.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"Healthcare professional with a B.S. in Biology and CCRC certification, bringing 1 year of clinical research experience. Screened 120+ potential participants for a Phase II cardiovascular trial, achieving an 85% consent-to-enrollment conversion rate. Proficient in REDCap, EPIC, and GCP guidelines."

**For Mid-Level:**
"Clinical Research Coordinator with 4 years of experience managing Phase I-III trials in endocrinology and neurology. Coordinated 6 concurrent studies with a combined enrollment of 280 participants, consistently meeting recruitment targets 2 weeks ahead of schedule. Zero audit findings across 3 sponsor monitoring visits."

**For Senior:**
"Senior Clinical Research Coordinator with 8+ years of experience overseeing multi-site trial operations at a top academic medical center. Managed a portfolio of 10 active studies with budgets totaling $2.1M. Trained and supervised a team of 4 coordinators while maintaining a 99% protocol compliance rate across all studies."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

### Study Coordination
- Patient screening, recruitment, and enrollment
- Informed consent administration
- Study visit scheduling and management
- Specimen collection and processing
- Study drug accountability and dispensing
- Subject retention strategies

### Regulatory & Documentation
- IRB/Ethics committee submissions
- Regulatory binder maintenance
- Adverse event and SAE reporting
- Source document creation and management
- Protocol deviation documentation
- FDA audit preparation

### Technical & Professional
- EDC systems (REDCap, Medidata, Velos)
- Electronic health records (EPIC, Cerner)
- Medical terminology
- Phlebotomy and vital signs
- Budget tracking and invoicing
- Multi-disciplinary team coordination

## Achievement-Focused Bullet Points

- "Enrolled 145 participants across 3 concurrent Phase II trials, exceeding recruitment targets by 20% in each study"
- "Maintained a 99.5% data entry accuracy rate across 8,500+ case report form entries over 18 months"
- "Coordinated 450+ study visits annually with a 96% participant retention rate across longitudinal studies"
- "Prepared and submitted 12 IRB protocols and 35 amendments with zero rejections or required revisions"
- "Reduced screen failure rate from 38% to 22% by implementing pre-screening questionnaires and eligibility checklists"
- "Managed study drug inventory for 5 active trials, maintaining 100% accountability with no discrepancies"

## Clinical Research Coordinator Resume Format & Template Tips

Choosing the right resume format is critical for healthcare roles:
- **Chronological resume format** — Best for clinical research coordinator candidates with steady career progression
- **Professional resume format** — Use clean section headers, consistent spacing, and a modern but readable font
- **ATS-friendly resume template** — Avoid graphics, tables, and columns that confuse applicant tracking systems
- **Resume length** — 1 page for entry-level / 1-2 pages for experienced / 2 pages for senior
- Save your resume as PDF to preserve your resume format across all devices and ATS platforms

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Not listing certifications** — CCRC, CCRP, or ACRP certifications significantly boost your credibility
2. **Omitting enrollment and retention numbers** — These are the most important metrics for CRC performance
3. **Ignoring therapeutic areas** — Always specify the disease areas and trial phases you have coordinated
4. **Being vague about regulatory work** — List specific IRB submissions, amendments, and audit outcomes
5. **Forgetting technical systems** — REDCap, EPIC, and EDC platform experience is essential to include
