## What Makes a Great Network Administrator Resume?

This network administrator resume example shows exactly what hiring managers want to see. The right resume format makes the difference between landing an interview and getting filtered out. Employers look for candidates who can demonstrate hands-on experience managing complex network infrastructures while maintaining uptime and security. Your resume should clearly articulate your certifications, the scale of networks you have managed, and measurable improvements you have delivered.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"Detail-oriented network administrator with CompTIA Network+ certification and 1 year of hands-on experience configuring routers, switches, and firewalls in a lab environment supporting 50+ devices. Reduced network downtime by 15% during internship through proactive monitoring and patch management."

**For Mid-Level:**
"Certified network administrator with 5 years of experience managing enterprise LAN/WAN infrastructure for 500+ users across 3 office locations. Achieved 99.95% uptime through implementation of redundant network architecture and automated monitoring. Holds CCNA and CompTIA Security+ certifications."

**For Senior:**
"Senior network administrator with 10+ years leading enterprise network operations supporting 2,000+ endpoints across 12 global sites. Spearheaded migration from legacy infrastructure to SD-WAN, reducing bandwidth costs by 35% and improving application performance by 40%. CCNP and CCIE certified."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

### Network Infrastructure
- LAN/WAN design and configuration
- Cisco and Juniper router/switch management
- VLAN segmentation and trunking
- Wireless network deployment (802.11ax)
- SD-WAN implementation
- Network cabling and rack management

### Security & Compliance
- Firewall configuration (Palo Alto, Fortinet, pfSense)
- VPN setup and management (IPSec, SSL)
- Intrusion detection/prevention systems (IDS/IPS)
- Network access control (NAC)
- SOC 2 and PCI DSS compliance
- Vulnerability scanning and remediation

### Monitoring & Administration
- SolarWinds, Nagios, PRTG monitoring
- Active Directory and Group Policy
- DHCP, DNS, and IPAM management
- PowerShell and Bash scripting
- Ticketing systems (ServiceNow, Jira)
- Documentation with Visio and Lucidchart

## Network Administrator Resume Format & Template Tips

Choosing the right resume format is critical for technology roles:
- **Chronological resume format** — Best for network administrator candidates with steady career progression
- **Professional resume format** — Use clean section headers, consistent spacing, and a modern but readable font
- **ATS-friendly resume template** — Avoid graphics, tables, and columns that confuse applicant tracking systems
- **Resume length** — 1 page for entry-level / 1-2 pages for experienced / 2 pages for senior
- Save your resume as PDF to preserve your resume format across all devices and ATS platforms

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Listing certifications without context** — Mention how you applied CCNA or CompTIA knowledge in real projects, not just that you hold them
2. **Ignoring uptime metrics** — Hiring managers expect specific uptime percentages and the scale of infrastructure you managed
3. **Using vague descriptions like "managed network"** — Quantify with user counts, device numbers, and site locations
4. **Omitting security experience** — Network security is non-negotiable; always highlight firewall, VPN, and compliance work
5. **Forgetting to mention monitoring tools** — Employers want to know the specific platforms you are proficient in
