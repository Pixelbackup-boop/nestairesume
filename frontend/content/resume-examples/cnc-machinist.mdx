## What Makes a Great CNC Machinist Resume?

CNC machinists create precision parts for aerospace, medical, automotive, and manufacturing. The role combines traditional machining knowledge with modern programming and automation skills. With +7% job growth and an average salary of $50,000, the CNC Machinist field offers solid career opportunities for skilled tradespeople. Your resume must communicate your certifications, hands-on experience, and ability to work safely and efficiently. This guide covers what employers look for when hiring CNC Machinist professionals.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level CNC Machinist:**
"Motivated CNC Machinist with hands-on training in CNC Programming and G-Code. Completed apprenticeship/training program with strong foundation in Blueprint Reading. Eager to apply skills in a professional environment with commitment to safety and quality work."

**For Experienced CNC Machinist:**
"Skilled CNC Machinist with 5+ years of experience in CNC Programming and G-Code. Proven track record of completing projects on time while maintaining excellent safety record. Experienced in Blueprint Reading with strong troubleshooting abilities."

**For Senior CNC Machinist:**
"Master CNC Machinist with 15+ years of experience leading crews and managing complex projects. Expert in CNC Programming, G-Code, and Blueprint Reading. Trained 20+ apprentices and maintained zero-incident safety record over past 5 years."

## CNC Machinist Resume Format & Template Tips

Trade resumes need to communicate competence quickly. Hiring managers often review dozens of applications—make yours stand out:

- **Certifications at the top** — License numbers, certification types, and expiration dates should be immediately visible. This is the first thing employers verify
- **Quantify your experience** — Years in trade, number of projects, production metrics, and safety record with specific numbers
- **Specify equipment and systems** — List specific brands, models, or systems you're experienced with. Generic descriptions don't differentiate you
- **Include safety record** — Incident-free years, safety training completed, and OSHA certifications demonstrate professionalism
- **Show progression** — Apprentice to journeyman to master, crew lead to foreman—show career growth
- **One page preferred** — Trades value efficiency. Keep it concise and relevant

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not specifying machine types and brands

Haas, Mazak, Okuma, DMG Mori—list specific machines you've operated. Include number of axes

### Missing tolerance capabilities

What precision can you hold? ±0.001", ±0.0005", tighter? Tolerance capability defines your skill level

### Ignoring programming experience

Setup-only vs programming capability matters. Include CAM software and G-code proficiency

### No quality metrics

First-pass yield, scrap rate, and on-time delivery demonstrate consistent quality. Include these metrics

### Not mentioning industry experience

Aerospace, medical, automotive have different requirements. Specify industries and certifications like AS9100



## Hiring Manager Tip

> **CNC Machinist resumes that show certifications, productivity, and safety record get prioritized.**

Tell me your tolerances and part complexity. "5-axis CNC machinist producing aerospace components to ±0.0002" tolerance with 99.7% first-pass yield" tells me your capability level. Include machines operated (Haas, Mazak, DMG Mori), programming software (Mastercam, Fusion), and industries served. Multi-axis and programming experience commands premium rates.


## Common CNC Machinist Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in CNC Machinist interviews, along with guidance on how to answer them:

### "What CNC machines have you operated?"

List specific brands, models, and axis configurations. Include both lathe and mill experience.

### "Describe your programming experience"

Discuss G-code proficiency, CAM software used, and level (setup only, modify programs, or program from scratch).

### "How do you ensure parts meet tolerance specifications?"

Cover in-process inspection, measurement tools, SPC, and response to out-of-spec conditions.

### "Walk me through your setup process for a new job"

Describe program verification, tool setup, first article inspection, and production release.

### "What industries have you worked in?"

Aerospace, medical, automotive have different standards. Discuss quality systems and certifications.
