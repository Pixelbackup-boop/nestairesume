## What Makes a Great Sheet Metal Worker Resume?

Sheet metal workers fabricate and install ductwork, roofing, and architectural metal. The trade combines shop fabrication with field installation, requiring precision and physical capability. With +4% job growth and an average salary of $53,000, the Sheet Metal Worker field offers solid career opportunities for skilled tradespeople. Your resume must communicate your certifications, hands-on experience, and ability to work safely and efficiently. This guide covers what employers look for when hiring Sheet Metal Worker professionals.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Sheet Metal Worker:**
"Motivated Sheet Metal Worker with hands-on training in HVAC Ductwork and Metal Fabrication. Completed apprenticeship/training program with strong foundation in Blueprint Reading. Eager to apply skills in a professional environment with commitment to safety and quality work."

**For Experienced Sheet Metal Worker:**
"Skilled Sheet Metal Worker with 5+ years of experience in HVAC Ductwork and Metal Fabrication. Proven track record of completing projects on time while maintaining excellent safety record. Experienced in Blueprint Reading with strong troubleshooting abilities."

**For Senior Sheet Metal Worker:**
"Master Sheet Metal Worker with 15+ years of experience leading crews and managing complex projects. Expert in HVAC Ductwork, Metal Fabrication, and Blueprint Reading. Trained 20+ apprentices and maintained zero-incident safety record over past 5 years."

## Sheet Metal Worker Resume Format & Template Tips

Trade resumes need to communicate competence quickly. Hiring managers often review dozens of applications—make yours stand out:

- **Certifications at the top** — License numbers, certification types, and expiration dates should be immediately visible. This is the first thing employers verify
- **Quantify your experience** — Years in trade, number of projects, production metrics, and safety record with specific numbers
- **Specify equipment and systems** — List specific brands, models, or systems you're experienced with. Generic descriptions don't differentiate you
- **Include safety record** — Incident-free years, safety training completed, and OSHA certifications demonstrate professionalism
- **Show progression** — Apprentice to journeyman to master, crew lead to foreman—show career growth
- **One page preferred** — Trades value efficiency. Keep it concise and relevant

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not distinguishing shop vs field experience

Fabrication and installation require different skills. Specify your experience in each area

### Missing project scale metrics

How much ductwork have you installed? Square footage of roofing? Numbers demonstrate experience

### Ignoring specialty applications

Hospital HVAC, commercial kitchens, or clean rooms require specialized knowledge. Highlight specialties

### No fabrication equipment listed

List plasma tables, press brakes, and other equipment you're proficient with

### Not mentioning union status if applicable

Union journeyman status is valuable in many markets. Include your union local



## Hiring Manager Tip

> **Sheet Metal Worker resumes that show certifications, productivity, and safety record get prioritized.**

Shop fabrication vs field installation are different focuses—be clear about your experience in each. "Journeyman sheet metal worker with 10 years HVAC ductwork fabrication and installation, completing 50+ commercial projects annually" shows volume and specialty. Include linear feet of ductwork installed, fabrication equipment operated, and any specialty installations (hospitals, clean rooms, kitchens).


## Common Sheet Metal Worker Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Sheet Metal Worker interviews, along with guidance on how to answer them:

### "What type of sheet metal work do you specialize in?"

Discuss HVAC, architectural, roofing, or industrial specialties and your experience level in each.

### "Describe your experience with ductwork fabrication"

Cover materials, equipment, pattern development, and assembly techniques.

### "How do you ensure proper airflow in HVAC installations?"

Discuss balancing, sizing, testing, and coordinating with HVAC technicians.

### "What fabrication equipment are you proficient with?"

List specific equipment: plasma tables, press brakes, rollers, shears, and any CNC equipment.

### "How do you handle working in occupied buildings?"

Discuss customer consideration, dust control, noise management, and scheduling around building use.
