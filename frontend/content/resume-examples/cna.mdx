## What Makes a Great CNA Resume?

This CNA resume example shows what nursing directors and healthcare hiring managers prioritize when filling certified nursing assistant positions. The right resume format emphasizes patient care skills, clinical certifications, and your ability to work compassionately under pressure. A well-crafted CNA resume example goes beyond listing care duties — it demonstrates your reliability, empathy, and measurable contributions to patient outcomes. Whether you work in hospitals, long-term care facilities, or home health settings, the proper resume format highlights both your clinical competence and interpersonal strengths. The strongest resume examples in this field feature patient-to-CNA ratios, satisfaction scores, and specific care procedures.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"State-certified Nursing Assistant with CNA license and 6 months of clinical rotation experience at a 120-bed skilled nursing facility. Provided daily care for 8-10 patients including vital signs, ADL assistance, and mobility support. CPR/BLS certified with a professional resume built on compassionate, patient-centered care."

**For Mid-Level:**
"Dedicated CNA with 3 years of experience in acute care and long-term care settings. Managed daily care for 12-15 patients, consistently earning 95%+ patient satisfaction scores. Skilled in EHR documentation, infection control protocols, and dementia care techniques. A professional resume that reflects dependable, high-quality patient support."

**For Senior:**
"Experienced Certified Nursing Assistant with 7+ years across hospital, rehabilitation, and hospice environments. Provided care for 15+ patients per shift, mentored 10 new CNAs, and maintained zero patient safety incidents over 3 consecutive years. Professional resume demonstrates advanced skills in wound care, catheter management, and emergency response."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

### Clinical Skills
- Vital signs monitoring (blood pressure, pulse, temperature, respiration)
- Activities of Daily Living (ADL) assistance
- Patient positioning and mobility assistance
- Wound care and dressing changes
- Catheter care and output monitoring

### Safety & Compliance
- Infection control and standard precautions
- Fall prevention protocols
- CPR/BLS certification
- HIPAA compliance
- Incident reporting and documentation

### Interpersonal Skills
- Patient communication and emotional support
- Family and visitor interaction
- Team collaboration with RNs and LPNs
- Cultural sensitivity and diversity awareness
- Time management across multiple patients

Feature these skills prominently on your resume template to align with the clinical competencies healthcare employers screen for during hiring.

These resume examples prove that even entry-level CNA work can be presented with measurable impact and clinical credibility.

## CNA Resume Format & Template Tips

Choosing the right resume format is critical for healthcare positions where accuracy and clarity reflect your professional standards:

- **Chronological resume format** — Best for CNAs with continuous healthcare experience, showing your most recent clinical role first with patient volumes and care specialties
- **Resume format for new CNAs** — If recently certified, use a combination resume format that leads with your CNA certification, clinical skills, and clinical rotation experience before work history
- **ATS-friendly resume template** — Hospitals and healthcare systems rely heavily on applicant tracking systems, so use clean formatting without graphics, text boxes, or multi-column layouts
- **Certification placement** — List your CNA certification, state license number, and CPR/BLS credentials near the top of your resume. This resume format ensures recruiters verify your qualifications immediately
- **Resume length** — Keep your CNA resume to 1 page, focusing on relevant clinical experience and measurable patient care outcomes
- Save as PDF to preserve your resume format across all healthcare hiring platforms

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Missing certification details** — Always include your CNA license number, issuing state, and expiration date at the top of your resume
2. **No patient ratios** — Healthcare employers want to know your patient load; always include the number of patients you cared for per shift
3. **Ignoring EHR proficiency** — Electronic Health Record systems like Epic, Cerner, and PointClickCare are standard tools; list your specific system experience
4. **Vague care descriptions** — "Helped patients" is too generic; specify the types of care you provided (ADL assistance, vital signs, wound care, mobility)
5. **Overlooking continuing education** — Dementia care certification, phlebotomy training, or advanced CNA coursework shows commitment to growth

## ATS Optimization for CNA Resumes

Healthcare applicant tracking systems are specifically designed to screen for clinical keywords and certifications. Your ats resume format must be optimized:

- Use exact terminology from the job posting — if it says "Activities of Daily Living," include that phrase rather than just "patient care"
- Structure your ats resume template with clear sections: Summary, Certifications, Clinical Skills, Work Experience, Education
- Include both full terms and abbreviations: "Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA)," "Basic Life Support (BLS)," "Electronic Health Records (EHR)," "Activities of Daily Living (ADL)"
- Place critical keywords like "patient care," "vital signs," "infection control," "ADL assistance," and "EHR documentation" in both your skills section and experience bullets
- Use plain-text formatting without tables or images — your ats resume must be parseable so every clinical qualification is captured accurately
