## What Makes a Great CNA Resume?

This CNA resume example is designed specifically for candidates who hold (or are about to earn) their state CNA certification. The abbreviation "CNA" is how most job postings and healthcare staffing agencies list these roles, and your resume needs to match that terminology exactly for ATS compatibility.

A strong CNA resume leads with your certification details — state, license number, and expiration date — because healthcare recruiters verify credentials before reading anything else. Unlike a general "nursing assistant" resume, a CNA resume must demonstrate that you have completed a state-approved training program (typically 75-120 hours) and passed both the written and clinical skills components of the state competency exam.

This guide focuses on how to present your clinical rotation hours, patient care competencies, and facility-specific experience in a format that passes both ATS screening and the 7-second recruiter scan that determines whether your resume gets a closer look.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"Recently certified CNA (State of [State], License #XXXXX, exp. XX/2027) with 80 hours of clinical rotation at [Facility Name], a 200-bed skilled nursing facility. Provided ADL assistance, vital signs monitoring, and mobility support for 8-10 residents per shift during clinicals. CPR/BLS certified. Seeking first full-time CNA position to apply patient-centered care training in a long-term care or hospital setting."

**For Mid-Level:**
"State-certified CNA with 3 years of bedside experience across acute care and long-term care environments. Currently caring for 12-15 patients per shift on a 40-bed medical-surgical unit, maintaining 96% patient satisfaction scores. Proficient in Epic EHR, infection control protocols, and dementia-specific care techniques. CNA certification current through 2027."

**For Senior:**
"Veteran CNA with 8+ years of clinical experience spanning hospital, rehabilitation, and hospice settings. Manage 15+ patients per shift, serve as CNA preceptor for new hires (trained 20+ CNAs), and maintain zero patient safety incidents across 3 consecutive years. Advanced competencies in wound care, catheter management, tracheostomy care, and end-of-life comfort measures. Preparing for LPN bridge program."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

### Clinical Skills
- Vital signs monitoring (blood pressure, pulse, temperature, respiration)
- Activities of Daily Living (ADL) assistance
- Patient positioning and mobility assistance
- Wound care and dressing changes
- Catheter care and output monitoring

### Safety & Compliance
- Infection control and standard precautions
- Fall prevention protocols
- CPR/BLS certification
- HIPAA compliance
- Incident reporting and documentation

### Interpersonal Skills
- Patient communication and emotional support
- Family and visitor interaction
- Team collaboration with RNs and LPNs
- Cultural sensitivity and diversity awareness
- Time management across multiple patients

Feature these skills prominently on your resume template to align with the clinical competencies healthcare employers screen for during hiring.

## Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) Resume Format & Template Tips

CNA resumes are scanned differently than RN or LPN resumes. Hiring managers at skilled nursing facilities and hospitals are looking for proof that you can handle a full patient load safely and independently:

- **State certification front and center** — List your CNA certification number, issuing state, and expiration date in a header section or right below your name. Facilities must verify active certification before scheduling interviews
- **Patient-to-CNA ratios and shift type** — "8–10 residents per shift in a 120-bed skilled nursing facility" or "12 patients on a med-surg unit, night shift" communicates your workload capacity and environment
- **ADL proficiency with specific counts** — Quantify activities of daily living: "Assisted 10 residents daily with bathing, dressing, feeding, and mobility transfers including Hoyer lift operation"
- **Vital signs and documentation accuracy** — "Recorded vital signs for 15 patients per shift with 100% EHR accuracy in Epic" proves you can handle the charting demands modern facilities require
- **Specialty training and additional certifications** — CPR/BLS, dementia care, wound care basics, or phlebotomy training differentiate you from other CNA candidates. List specific training hours completed

## Hiring Manager Tip

> **When I'm hiring CNAs, I look at three things in under 10 seconds: active certification, facility type, and patient load.**

At a skilled nursing facility, I need to know you've handled a full assignment — 8 to 12 residents per shift — without constant supervision. I skip resumes that list "assisted with ADLs" without any numbers. Tell me how many residents you managed, what shift you worked, and whether you worked in long-term care, rehab, or acute settings. If you floated between units, say so — it shows adaptability. And always list your state certification number and expiration. I've tossed otherwise solid resumes because I couldn't verify certification from the document itself.

## Common Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) interviews, along with guidance on how to answer them:

### "How do you handle a combative or agitated resident during personal care?"

Describe your de-escalation approach: speaking calmly, giving the resident space, offering choices ("Would you like to wash your face first or brush your teeth?"), and knowing when to step away and return later. Mention documenting the behavior and reporting to the charge nurse.

### "A resident's family member complains that their parent isn't being bathed often enough. What do you do?"

Show professionalism: listen without becoming defensive, explain the bathing schedule and care plan, document the concern, and escalate to the charge nurse. Emphasize empathy — families are often stressed and need reassurance their loved one is receiving attentive care.

### "You notice a change in a resident's skin during a transfer. What are your next steps?"

Walk through your assessment and reporting chain: describe what you observed (redness, open area, bruising), how you positioned the resident safely, who you reported to (charge nurse), and how you documented it in the EHR. Mention staging awareness and pressure injury prevention protocols.

### "How do you prioritize your assignments when you have 10 residents to care for and a two-hour window?"

Explain your workflow: check on highest-acuity residents first, group tasks by proximity, coordinate with fellow CNAs for two-person transfers, and communicate with the charge nurse if a resident's needs change. Include time-specific examples from your experience.

### "Tell me about a time you had to use a mechanical lift. What safety checks do you perform?"

Detail the Hoyer or sit-to-stand lift procedure: checking weight limits, inspecting sling integrity, ensuring brakes are locked, positioning the sling correctly, maintaining eye contact and communication with the resident throughout, and having a second person assist. Mention your facility's specific lift policy.


## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Missing certification details

Always include your CNA license number, issuing state, and expiration date at the top of your resume
### No patient ratios

Healthcare employers want to know your patient load; always include the number of patients you cared for per shift
### Ignoring EHR proficiency

Electronic Health Record systems like Epic, Cerner, and PointClickCare are standard tools; list your specific system experience
### Vague care descriptions

"Helped patients" is too generic; specify the types of care you provided (ADL assistance, vital signs, wound care, mobility)
### Overlooking continuing education

Dementia care certification, phlebotomy training, or advanced CNA coursework shows commitment to growth

## ATS Optimization for CNA Resumes

Healthcare applicant tracking systems are specifically designed to screen for clinical keywords and certifications. Your ats resume format must be optimized:

- Use exact terminology from the job posting — if it says "Activities of Daily Living," include that phrase rather than just "patient care"
- Structure your ats resume template with clear sections: Summary, Certifications, Clinical Skills, Work Experience, Education
- Include both full terms and abbreviations: "Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA)," "Basic Life Support (BLS)," "Electronic Health Records (EHR)," "Activities of Daily Living (ADL)"
- Place critical keywords like "patient care," "vital signs," "infection control," "ADL assistance," and "EHR documentation" in both your skills section and experience bullets
- Use plain-text formatting without tables or images — your ats resume must be parseable so every clinical qualification is captured accurately
