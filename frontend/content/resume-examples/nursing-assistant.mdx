## What Makes a Great Nursing Assistant Resume?

This nursing assistant resume example highlights what hospitals, nursing homes, and long-term care facilities want to see from CNA candidates. The right resume format foregrounds your hands-on patient care experience, clinical competencies, and compassionate bedside manner. A strong nursing assistant resume example balances technical skills like vital signs monitoring and infection control with interpersonal abilities that demonstrate genuine commitment to patient well-being. Hiring managers scan for certifications, patient-to-staff ratios handled, and measurable care outcomes — your resume format should make these credentials easy to identify at a glance.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"Certified Nursing Assistant with clinical training in patient care, vital signs monitoring, and ADL support completed through a state-approved CNA program with 120+ hours of clinical experience. Skilled in safe patient transfers, basic wound care, and infection control protocols. A professional resume reflecting a deep commitment to compassionate, patient-centered care."

**For Mid-Level:**
"Dedicated Nursing Assistant with 3 years of experience providing direct patient care in a 120-bed long-term care facility. Managed daily care for 10-12 patients including bathing, feeding, mobility assistance, and vital signs documentation. Maintained 100% compliance on infection control audits for 8 consecutive quarters. This professional resume demonstrates reliable clinical skills and a patient-first approach."

**For Senior:**
"Senior Nursing Assistant with 8+ years of experience across acute care, rehabilitation, and memory care settings. Served as CNA preceptor training 20+ new nursing assistants in patient care protocols, safety procedures, and electronic documentation. Recognized with Facility Caregiver of the Year award for outstanding patient satisfaction scores and zero incident reports over a 2-year period."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

When building your nursing assistant resume template, include a targeted mix of clinical and interpersonal skills.

### Clinical Skills
- Activities of Daily Living (ADL) support: bathing, dressing, grooming, feeding
- Vital signs monitoring: blood pressure, pulse, temperature, respiration, oxygen saturation
- Safe patient transfers and mobility assistance (Hoyer lift, gait belt)
- Infection control and standard precautions (PPE, hand hygiene)
- Basic wound care and skin integrity assessments

### Communication & Documentation Skills
- Patient observation and reporting to nursing staff
- Electronic Health Record (EHR) documentation
- Medical terminology comprehension
- Family communication and emotional support
- Care plan adherence and shift reporting

### Certifications
- Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) — state licensure
- CPR/BLS (Basic Life Support) certification
- First Aid certification
- Dementia and Alzheimer's care training
- Bloodborne Pathogens training

A clear resume template should group these skills for quick identification by hiring managers and screening systems.

## Achievement-Focused Bullet Points

Use these resume examples as models for quantifying your patient care impact:

- "Provided daily ADL support to 10-12 patients in a 150-bed skilled nursing facility, maintaining 98% patient satisfaction scores across quarterly surveys"
- "Monitored and documented vital signs for 15+ patients per shift with 100% accuracy in electronic health record entries"

- "Maintained 100% compliance with infection control protocols across 12 state health department inspections"
- "Trained and mentored 8 new CNAs on patient care procedures, documentation standards, and facility safety protocols"

These resume examples transform routine caregiving duties into compelling, measurable accomplishments.

## Nursing Assistant Resume Format & Template Tips

Selecting the proper resume format ensures your caregiving qualifications stand out to healthcare employers:

- **Chronological resume format** — Best for nursing assistants with consistent healthcare experience, listing your most recent care facility first to show progression from entry-level to specialized or senior roles
- **Resume format for career changers** — If transitioning from home health aide, personal care aide, or another caregiving role, use a combination resume format that leads with transferable patient care skills and certifications
- **ATS-friendly resume template** — Healthcare facilities often use applicant tracking systems. Use standard section headers like "Experience," "Skills," and "Certifications." Avoid tables, graphics, or multi-column layouts that confuse automated screening
- **Resume length** — Keep to 1 page for entry and mid-level nursing assistants; senior CNAs with specialized training may extend to 2 pages
- Save your resume as a PDF to preserve the resume format across all platforms and devices

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Omitting your CNA certification number and state** — Facilities must verify licensure; include your certification number and issuing state prominently
2. **Using vague descriptions** — Specify patient-to-staff ratios, bed counts, and care settings rather than generic "provided patient care" statements
3. **Forgetting specialized training** — Dementia care, hospice, wound care, and rehabilitation experience should be highlighted as differentiators
4. **Ignoring compliance and safety records** — Zero-incident streaks, audit scores, and infection control compliance are powerful proof points
5. **Neglecting soft skills** — Empathy, patience, and communication are what distinguish great nursing assistants from adequate ones

## ATS Optimization for Nursing Assistant Resumes

To pass applicant tracking systems, structure your nursing assistant resume with an ats resume format that incorporates keywords from the job posting. Include terms like "patient care," "ADL support," "vital signs monitoring," "infection control," "mobility assistance," and "CPR/BLS" throughout your experience section. Use an ats resume template with clean formatting — single-column layout, standard fonts, and no embedded images. Match your skills section directly to the job description requirements and spell out abbreviations at least once (e.g., "Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA)") so the ATS can parse them correctly.
