## What Makes a Great Prep Cook Resume?

Prep cooks set up line cooks for success by preparing ingredients before service. Speed, consistency, and organization are essential. With +16% job growth and an average salary of $30,000, Prep Cook positions offer opportunities for those passionate about food service. Your resume must communicate your skills, experience, and reliability. This guide covers what food service employers look for when hiring.


Kitchen managers evaluate prep cooks on consistency, knife skills, and the ability to maintain production schedules. Your resume should quantify daily prep volume, highlight your knowledge of mise en place standards, and reference specific cuisines or techniques that demonstrate your culinary foundation.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Prep Cook:**
"Enthusiastic Prep Cook with training in Food Preparation and Knife Skills. Strong work ethic with commitment to food safety and customer satisfaction. Eager to contribute to a fast-paced food service team."

**For Experienced Prep Cook:**
"Skilled Prep Cook with 3+ years experience in high-volume food service. Proficient in Food Preparation and Recipe Following. Known for reliability, attention to detail, and positive customer interactions."

**For Senior Prep Cook:**
"Accomplished Prep Cook with 8+ years in food service operations. Expert in Food Preparation, Knife Skills, and team leadership. Track record of improving efficiency and maintaining quality standards."

Quantify your contributions:

- "Consistently maintained food safety standards with zero health code violations"

- "Reduced food waste by 15% through improved inventory management"
- "Trained 5 new team members on food safety and service procedures"

## Prep Cook Resume Format Tips

Food service resumes should be clear and easy to scan:

- **Certifications first** — ServSafe, food handler's permits are often required
- **Quantify experience** — Covers served, volume handled, customer counts
- **Show reliability** — Attendance record, schedule flexibility
- **Include relevant skills** — Food safety, POS systems, specific equipment
- **Keep it to one page** — Concise and focused on relevant experience

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not mentioning volume and restaurant type

How many covers does your prep support? What type of cuisine? Context matters

### Missing knife skill details

Classic cuts, speed, consistency—describe your knife proficiency

### Ignoring organization abilities

Walk-in organization, FIFO, labeling—organization prevents waste and enables service

### No food safety emphasis

Proper storage, temperatures, cross-contamination prevention—safety is fundamental

### Not showing advancement interest

Prep cook is often an entry point. Show desire to learn and grow



## Hiring Manager Tip

> **Prep Cook resumes that demonstrate reliability, food safety knowledge, and customer service skills get prioritized.**

"Prep cook completing prep lists for 150-cover restaurant, responsible for vegetable prep, sauce production, and protein portioning." Show volume handled, prep types, and reliability. Knife skills and organization are key—if you can julienne onions perfectly at speed and keep the walk-in organized, you're valuable.


## Common Prep Cook Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Prep Cook interviews:

### "Describe your knife skills"

Discuss classic cuts you're comfortable with and your speed/consistency.

### "How do you prioritize a long prep list?"

Show organizational thinking and understanding of what needs to happen first.

### "What do you do if you're running behind on prep?"

Communication with chef, prioritization, and problem-solving.

### "Explain FIFO and why it matters"

First In First Out prevents waste and ensures food safety.

### "What do you want to learn in this kitchen?"

Show eagerness to develop skills and advance.
