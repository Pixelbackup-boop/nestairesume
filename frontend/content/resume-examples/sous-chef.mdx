## What Makes a Great Sous Chef Resume?

Sous chefs are the executive chef's right hand, managing daily kitchen operations and ensuring consistent food quality during service. With +6% job growth and an average salary of $55,000, Sous Chef positions offer opportunities for those passionate about food service. Your resume must communicate your skills, experience, and reliability. This guide covers what food service employers look for when hiring.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Sous Chef:**
"Enthusiastic Sous Chef with training in Kitchen Operations and Line Management. Strong work ethic with commitment to food safety and customer satisfaction. Eager to contribute to a fast-paced food service team."

**For Experienced Sous Chef:**
"Skilled Sous Chef with 3+ years experience in high-volume food service. Proficient in Kitchen Operations and Food Preparation. Known for reliability, attention to detail, and positive customer interactions."

**For Senior Sous Chef:**
"Accomplished Sous Chef with 8+ years in food service operations. Expert in Kitchen Operations, Line Management, and team leadership. Track record of improving efficiency and maintaining quality standards."

## Achievement-Focused Bullet Points

Quantify your contributions:

- "Consistently maintained food safety standards with zero health code violations"
- "Served 200+ customers daily while maintaining 95% satisfaction ratings"
- "Reduced food waste by 15% through improved inventory management"
- "Trained 5 new team members on food safety and service procedures"
- "Recognized for perfect attendance over 12-month period"

## Sous Chef Resume Format Tips

Food service resumes should be clear and easy to scan:

- **Certifications first** — ServSafe, food handler's permits are often required
- **Quantify experience** — Covers served, volume handled, customer counts
- **Show reliability** — Attendance record, schedule flexibility
- **Include relevant skills** — Food safety, POS systems, specific equipment
- **Keep it to one page** — Concise and focused on relevant experience

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not demonstrating leadership readiness

Show you can run the kitchen independently. Include examples of managing service without the executive chef

### Missing operational metrics

Covers per service, prep volume, staff size—quantify your operational experience

### Ignoring administrative skills

Ordering, scheduling, inventory—sous chefs handle significant admin work. Include this experience

### No mention of training or mentoring

Sous chefs develop line cooks. Include training and development contributions

### Only listing cooking skills

Balance culinary abilities with management and operational capabilities



## Hiring Manager Tip

> **Sous Chef resumes that demonstrate reliability, food safety knowledge, and customer service skills get prioritized.**

Sous chefs need to run service seamlessly when the exec is absent. "Sous chef managing 12-person kitchen, responsible for daily prep, line management, and $400K monthly food orders." Show you can handle both culinary execution and administrative duties. Include covers per service, cuisine style, and any signature dishes you've contributed.


## Common Sous Chef Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Sous Chef interviews:

### "How do you prepare for a busy service?"

Discuss mise en place, staff briefings, anticipating needs, and backup plans.

### "Describe how you handle a kitchen that's falling behind during service"

Show calm leadership, problem-solving, and communication under pressure.

### "How do you train new line cooks?"

Discuss onboarding, standards, observation, and feedback processes.

### "Tell me about your experience with inventory management"

Cover ordering, receiving, storage, and waste reduction.

### "What's your goal for your culinary career?"

Show ambition while demonstrating commitment to the sous chef role.
