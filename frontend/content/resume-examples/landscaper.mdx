## What Makes a Great Landscaper Resume?

Landscapers create and maintain outdoor spaces. The work is seasonal in many areas, physically demanding, and increasingly technical with irrigation and lighting systems. With +5% job growth and an average salary of $37,000, the Landscaper field offers solid career opportunities for skilled tradespeople. Your resume must communicate your certifications, hands-on experience, and ability to work safely and efficiently. This guide covers what employers look for when hiring Landscaper professionals.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Landscaper:**
"Motivated Landscaper with hands-on training in Lawn Care and Planting. Completed apprenticeship/training program with strong foundation in Irrigation. Eager to apply skills in a professional environment with commitment to safety and quality work."

**For Experienced Landscaper:**
"Skilled Landscaper with 5+ years of experience in Lawn Care and Planting. Proven track record of completing projects on time while maintaining excellent safety record. Experienced in Irrigation with strong troubleshooting abilities."

**For Senior Landscaper:**
"Master Landscaper with 15+ years of experience leading crews and managing complex projects. Expert in Lawn Care, Planting, and Irrigation. Trained 20+ apprentices and maintained zero-incident safety record over past 5 years."


## Salary & Job Outlook

Landscaper professionals earn a median annual salary of approximately **$37,000**, with experienced professionals and those with specialized certifications earning significantly more. Employment for this occupation is projected to grow **+5%** over the next decade.

## Landscaper Resume Format & Template Tips

Trade resumes need to communicate competence quickly. Hiring managers often review dozens of applications—make yours stand out:

- **Certifications at the top** — License numbers, certification types, and expiration dates should be immediately visible. This is the first thing employers verify
- **Quantify your experience** — Years in trade, number of projects, production metrics, and safety record with specific numbers
- **Specify equipment and systems** — List specific brands, models, or systems you're experienced with. Generic descriptions don't differentiate you
- **Include safety record** — Incident-free years, safety training completed, and OSHA certifications demonstrate professionalism
- **Show progression** — Apprentice to journeyman to master, crew lead to foreman—show career growth
- **One page preferred** — Trades value efficiency. Keep it concise and relevant

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not specifying maintenance vs installation

These are different career paths. Be clear about your focus and experience

### Missing account or project volume

How many accounts do you maintain? How many installations per season? Numbers demonstrate capability

### Ignoring certifications

Pesticide applicator, irrigation, CDL—include all relevant certifications

### No equipment proficiency listed

List equipment you're proficient with: mowers, skid steers, trenchers, etc.

### Not mentioning plant knowledge

Knowing plants, their care, and regional adaptability is valuable knowledge to highlight



## Hiring Manager Tip

> **Landscaper resumes that show certifications, productivity, and safety record get prioritized.**

Maintenance vs installation are different skill sets—tell me which you do. "Landscape foreman managing 40 residential maintenance accounts with 95% retention rate" or "Hardscape installer completing 20+ paver patios annually, experienced in retaining walls and drainage." Include pesticide applicator license if you have it—it's often required for chemical applications.


## Common Landscaper Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Landscaper interviews, along with guidance on how to answer them:

### "What type of landscaping do you specialize in?"

Discuss maintenance vs installation, residential vs commercial, and your experience level.

### "Describe your experience with irrigation systems"

Cover installation, troubleshooting, and programming. Irrigation is a growing specialty.

### "How do you manage multiple maintenance accounts?"

Discuss routing, scheduling, and maintaining quality across all accounts.

### "What equipment are you experienced operating?"

List commercial mowers, skid steers, mini excavators, and other equipment.

### "How do you handle plant selection for different conditions?"

Demonstrate horticultural knowledge—sun/shade, soil, climate zone considerations.
