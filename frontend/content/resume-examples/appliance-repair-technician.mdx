## What Makes a Great Appliance Repair Technician Resume?

Appliance repair technicians fix the machines households depend on daily. The role combines technical diagnosis, customer service, and efficient field service operations. With +3% job growth and an average salary of $44,000, the Appliance Repair Technician field offers solid career opportunities for skilled tradespeople. Your resume must communicate your certifications, hands-on experience, and ability to work safely and efficiently. This guide covers what employers look for when hiring Appliance Repair Technician professionals.

## Appliance Repair Technician Resume Format & Template Tips

Service managers and dispatch leads scan an appliance repair technician resume for two things first: whether your EPA 608 status lets you legally touch a sealed system, and whether you can close calls on the first visit. Build the page so both answers jump out before they read a single bullet:

- **Lead with EPA 608 and the type** — "EPA 608 Universal" belongs in your header next to your name, not buried under education. Field service companies cannot dispatch you on refrigeration sealed-system work without it, so it's a hard screen
- **Put a "Brands Serviced" line near the top** — Whirlpool, GE, Samsung, LG, Bosch, Sub-Zero, Viking. Manufacturer authorization (factory-trained on a brand) is worth its own callout because warranty dispatch routes by brand certification
- **Quantify the route metrics that matter** — calls completed per day (6–8), first-call completion rate, callback/recall rate, and average customer rating. These are the numbers a service manager pulls from your file every quarter, so put them in your bullets
- **Separate residential from commercial** — commercial/restaurant refrigeration and laundromat work pays more and screens differently. State which you've done and on what equipment (walk-ins, ice machines, commercial dishwashers)
- **Show diagnostic depth, not just "repaired appliances"** — name the systems: sealed systems, defrost circuits, inverter compressors, control boards, gas valves. This signals you diagnose rather than swap parts
- **Note van stock and parts ownership** — a tech who carries common parts and uses a parts-lookup system (like SAMS or manufacturer portals) finishes more calls in one trip, which is exactly what raises your value
- **One page, reverse-chronological** — list your service territory and whether you ran your own route or worked dispatch

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not listing manufacturer certifications

Whirlpool, Samsung, LG, GE certifications show brand-specific expertise. List all manufacturer training

### Missing service metrics

Calls per day, first-call completion rate, customer ratings—these demonstrate productivity and quality

### Ignoring EPA certification

EPA 608 is required for refrigeration work. Include your certification type

### No differentiation of appliance types

Refrigeration, laundry, cooking, HVAC—specify which appliances you're most experienced with

### Not mentioning parts knowledge

Identifying and ordering parts is key. Include experience with parts systems and inventory

Include specific brands you're certified on (Whirlpool, Samsung, LG, GE)—manufacturer training shows capability. Commercial appliance experience pays more than residential.


## Common Appliance Repair Technician Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Appliance Repair Technician interviews, along with guidance on how to answer them:

### "Walk me through diagnosing a refrigerator that's not cooling"

Describe systematic diagnosis: sealed system, electrical, defrost system, controls. Show methodical approach.

### "What brands are you most experienced with?"

Discuss manufacturer training and brands you've worked on most.

### "How do you handle parts not in stock?"

Discuss parts identification, ordering processes, and customer communication about delays.

### "Describe your customer service approach"

You're in people's homes. Discuss communication, cleanliness, and managing expectations.

### "What is your first-call completion rate?"

Be honest. Discuss factors that affect completion and how you prepare for calls.
