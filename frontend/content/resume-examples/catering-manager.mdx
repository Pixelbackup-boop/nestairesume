## What Makes a Great Catering Manager Resume?

Catering managers oversee off-premise food service for events, managing sales, planning, and execution. Success requires sales ability and operational excellence. With +10% job growth and an average salary of $55,000, Catering Manager positions offer opportunities for those passionate about food service. Your resume must communicate your skills, experience, and reliability. This guide covers what food service employers look for when hiring.


Catering companies and event venues assess managers on event execution, client satisfaction, and revenue management. Feature specific event sizes coordinated, client retention rates, and revenue managed per quarter to demonstrate your ability to deliver seamless events while meeting financial targets.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Catering Manager:**
"Enthusiastic Catering Manager with training in Event Planning and Sales. Strong work ethic with commitment to food safety and customer satisfaction. Eager to contribute to a fast-paced food service team."

**For Experienced Catering Manager:**
"Skilled Catering Manager with 3+ years experience in high-volume food service. Proficient in Event Planning and Client Relations. Known for reliability, attention to detail, and positive customer interactions."

**For Senior Catering Manager:**
"Accomplished Catering Manager with 8+ years in food service operations. Expert in Event Planning, Sales, and team leadership. Track record of improving efficiency and maintaining quality standards."

## Catering Manager Resume Format & Template Tips

Catering is a revenue role wearing a chef's coat, so a director or venue GM reads your resume for two things at once: dollars booked and events executed cleanly. Format to prove both:

- **Lead with revenue and rebooking** — Open with the numbers that close interviews: annual catering revenue managed, average check or per-event value, sales growth, and client rebooking/referral rate. A summary line like "$1.8M annual catering revenue across 220 events, 92% client retention" earns the read
- **Build a two-column achievement layout** — Pair sales wins (proposals closed, upsell rate, new accounts) against operational wins (events delivered on budget, on-time service, zero-incident execution). Hiring managers reject candidates who are all sales and no execution, or vice versa
- **Segment events by type and size** — Break experience into corporate, weddings/social, and nonprofit/galas, with guest-count ranges for each. A 40-person plated dinner and a 600-guest buffet are different skill sets, and venues hire for the mix they run
- **Surface budget and vendor authority** — State the budgets you owned, vendors and rentals you negotiated, and staff headcount you scheduled per event. This signals P&L responsibility, which separates a manager from a coordinator
- **Place ServSafe Manager and event credentials up top** — ServSafe Manager, CMP, or TIPS/alcohol-service certification belong in a header band; for off-premise work, note your familiarity with permits, BEOs, and timeline/event-order documents
- **Two pages is acceptable here** — Unlike line-level food service roles, a seasoned catering manager can run to two pages to show event portfolio depth, but keep signature events and revenue figures on page one

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not showing revenue responsibility

Annual revenue, average event size, sales growth—catering is a revenue-driven business

### Missing event volume and types

Events per month, size range, corporate vs social—show your experience breadth

### Ignoring client relationship metrics

Rebooking rate, referrals, client feedback—relationships drive catering success

### No operational execution examples

Balance sales skills with examples of flawless event execution

### Not mentioning team coordination

Catering requires coordinating many moving parts. Show leadership skills



## Hiring Manager Tip

> **Catering Manager resumes that demonstrate reliability, food safety knowledge, and customer service skills get prioritized.**

"Catering manager generating $1.5M annual revenue across 200+ events, maintaining 90% client rebooking rate." Show revenue generated, event volume, and client satisfaction. Include event types (corporate, weddings, social), size range, and any signature events. Sales ability and operational execution must both be evident.


## Common Catering Manager Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Catering Manager interviews:

### "Tell me about your sales approach for catering"

Discuss lead generation, consultations, proposals, and closing.

### "Describe a challenging event and how you handled it"

Show problem-solving, adaptability, and maintaining client satisfaction.

### "How do you ensure consistent quality across multiple events?"

Cover staffing, training, checklists, and quality control systems.

### "What's the largest event you've managed?"

Discuss scope, challenges, and successful execution.

### "How do you handle a client request you can't accommodate?"

Show client service skills while managing expectations honestly.
