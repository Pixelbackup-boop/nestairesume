## What Makes a Great Production Assistant Resume?

This production assistant resume example shows exactly what hiring managers want to see. The right resume format makes the difference between landing an interview and getting filtered out. Production assistants are the backbone of film, television, and media productions, handling everything from logistics to on-set coordination. Your resume should demonstrate reliability, adaptability, and the ability to keep productions running smoothly under tight deadlines and high-pressure conditions.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"Enthusiastic production assistant with film school training and experience on 5 independent short film productions. Managed call sheets, coordinated talent schedules, and handled equipment setup for crews of 15-25 people. Known for punctuality, problem-solving, and ability to anticipate production needs."

**For Mid-Level:**
"Experienced production assistant with 3 years of work on 12 film and television productions including 2 network series and 4 feature films. Coordinated daily logistics for 80-person crews, managed background talent, and oversaw equipment inventory worth $200K. Promoted to key PA within first year on set."

**For Senior:**
"Veteran production assistant and production coordinator with 7+ years across 30+ film, television, and commercial productions. Managed production office operations for $5M feature film, coordinating schedules, permits, and vendor relationships. Transitioned from set PA to production office management, overseeing 4 junior PAs and day-to-day production logistics."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

### On-Set Operations
- Call sheet creation and distribution
- Talent and crew check-in management
- Background/extras coordination
- Set lockup and crowd control
- Walkie-talkie communication protocols
- Continuity and script supervision support

### Production Logistics
- Equipment inventory and transport
- Location scouting and permit coordination
- Catering and craft services arrangement
- Vehicle and parking management
- Prop and wardrobe organization
- Weather contingency planning

### Administrative & Technical
- Production scheduling software (Movie Magic, StudioBinder)
- Script breakdown and sides preparation
- Petty cash and expense tracking
- Camera and lighting equipment familiarity
- Google Workspace and Microsoft Office
- Social media documentation and BTS content

## Achievement-Focused Bullet Points

- "Served as key PA on 3 network television episodes, coordinating daily schedules for 75-person cast and crew across 8 locations"
- "Managed call sheet distribution and check-in process for 120 background extras during 4-day crowd scene shoot"
- "Coordinated equipment logistics for $200K worth of camera, lighting, and grip gear across 15-day indie film production"
- "Organized location permits, parking plans, and police coordination for 8 exterior shoots in downtown metropolitan areas"
- "Maintained production binder with daily reports, expense logs, and continuity notes across 30-day feature film schedule"
- "Received offer to return for 3 consecutive seasons of reality TV series based on reliability, work ethic, and crew feedback"

## Production Assistant Resume Format & Template Tips

Choosing the right resume format is critical for media roles:
- **Chronological resume format** — Best for production assistant candidates with steady career progression
- **Professional resume format** — Use clean section headers, consistent spacing, and a modern but readable font
- **ATS-friendly resume template** — Avoid graphics, tables, and columns that confuse applicant tracking systems
- **Resume length** — 1 page for entry-level / 1-2 pages for experienced / 2 pages for senior
- Save your resume as PDF to preserve your resume format across all devices and ATS platforms

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Not listing specific productions** — Name the shows, films, or studios you worked with to demonstrate credible experience
2. **Ignoring crew size and budget scale** — Production scale indicates your comfort level with different complexity levels
3. **Leaving off transferable skills** — Restaurant, event, and retail experience shows the hustle and multitasking PAs need
4. **Using a generic objective statement** — Tailor your summary to the specific type of production (film, TV, commercial, digital)
5. **Forgetting references and recommendations** — The film industry runs on reputation, so mention that references are available from named productions
