## What Makes a Great Occupational Therapist Resume?

Occupational therapists help patients perform daily activities and adapt to disabilities affecting their independence. The field uniquely focuses on meaningful activities—from self-care to work tasks—making OT essential in rehabilitation settings.

Healthcare employers evaluate Occupational Therapist candidates on specific credentials, clinical competencies, and demonstrated outcomes. With +12% projected job growth and an average salary of $93,000, competition for top Occupational Therapist positions requires a resume that immediately communicates your qualifications and value. This guide covers the specific sections, metrics, and formatting that healthcare recruiters look for when reviewing Occupational Therapist applications.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Occupational Therapist:**
"Recently certified Occupational Therapist with hands-on clinical experience in adl training and cognitive rehabilitation. Completed training with focus on adaptive equipment and patient-centered care. OT License certified with strong foundation in healthcare protocols and safety procedures."

**For Experienced Occupational Therapist:**
"Dedicated Occupational Therapist with 5+ years of experience in adl training and cognitive rehabilitation. Consistently maintained high patient satisfaction scores while managing complex cases. Proficient in sensory integration with proven track record of quality outcomes and team collaboration."

**For Senior Occupational Therapist:**
"Accomplished Occupational Therapist with 10+ years of progressive experience leading adl training initiatives. Expert in adaptive equipment with demonstrated success improving departmental outcomes by 25%. Mentor to junior staff with comprehensive knowledge of splinting and regulatory compliance."

Occupational Therapist professionals earn a median annual salary of approximately **$93,000**, with compensation varying based on experience, location, and work setting. Employment for this occupation is projected to grow **+12%** over the next decade, faster than the average for all occupations due to increasing healthcare demand.

**Sources:** Salary estimates are based on data from the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/occupational-therapists.htm), [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/occupational-therapist-salary-SRCH_KO0,22.htm), and [PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Occupational_Therapist/Salary). Actual compensation varies based on geographic location, facility type, certifications, and years of experience.

## Occupational Therapist Resume Format & Template Tips

Occupational Therapist resumes require specific attention to credentials and clinical experience. Here are formatting guidelines tailored to this role:

- **Credentials first** — List your OT License, NBCOT Certification immediately after your name. Healthcare recruiters verify credentials before reading anything else.
- **Quantify your experience** — Include patient volumes, success rates, and quality metrics. Numbers demonstrate competence better than generic descriptions.
- **Highlight relevant certifications** — Create a dedicated section for certifications with expiration dates. Keep them current and prominently displayed.
- **Use clinical terminology** — Mirror the language from job postings. Include specific equipment, systems, and protocols you're trained on.
- **One page for <5 years experience** — Focus on your most relevant and recent experience. Senior professionals may use two pages for extensive accomplishments.
- **Include continuing education** — Healthcare evolves rapidly. Show your commitment to staying current with recent training and certifications.

## Hiring Manager Tip

> **OT resumes should highlight your specialty area (hand therapy, pediatrics, mental health, geriatrics) and specific populations served.**

 Include functional outcome improvements, discharge to home rates, and adaptive equipment expertise. Mention any specialized certifications like CHT or sensory integration training.

## Common Occupational Therapist Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is essential for landing your Occupational Therapist role. Here are questions frequently asked in Occupational Therapist interviews:

### "Describe your approach to a patient with multiple functional limitations."

Discuss prioritization, client-centered goal setting, and how you address the most meaningful activities first.

### "How do you incorporate adaptive equipment into treatment plans?"

Show knowledge of equipment options, training patients, and advocating for insurance coverage.

### "Describe your experience with cognitive rehabilitation."

Discuss specific approaches, assessment tools, and populations (stroke, TBI, dementia).

### "How do you collaborate with the interdisciplinary team?"

Give examples of working with PT, SLP, nursing, and physicians on patient goals.

### "What is your experience with home safety assessments and modifications?"

Discuss evaluation process, recommendation development, and follow-through on modifications.

## ATS Optimization for Occupational Therapist Resumes

Healthcare ATS systems screen for specific credentials, certifications, and clinical keywords. Missing key terms can disqualify an otherwise strong application.

- Include exact credential abbreviations: "OT License", "NBCOT Certification"
- Use clinical terminology from job postings: "occupational therapist", "OT", "OTR", "ADL", "activities of daily living"
- List specific equipment, systems, or protocols you're experienced with
- Include both full terms and abbreviations for searchability
- Mention specific patient populations and care settings
