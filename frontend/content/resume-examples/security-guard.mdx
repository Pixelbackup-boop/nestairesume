## What Makes a Great Security Guard Resume?

This security guard resume example shows exactly what hiring managers at security firms, corporate facilities, and property management companies want to see. The right resume format makes the difference between landing interviews and getting filtered out. A strong security guard resume demonstrates vigilance, reliability, and the ability to handle emergencies calmly and professionally. Employers want to see your licensing credentials, incident response capabilities, and a proven track record of maintaining safe environments.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"Licensed security professional with 1 year of experience providing access control and surveillance monitoring for a 500,000 sq. ft. commercial property. Completed 1,200+ incident-free patrol shifts. CPR/First Aid and OSHA certified with strong observation and communication skills. A professional resume reflecting dedication to safety and vigilance."

**For Mid-Level:**
"Security Guard with 5 years of experience in corporate and event security settings, managing access control for facilities with 1,000+ daily visitors. Responded to and resolved 50+ security incidents with zero escalations to law enforcement. Professional resume showcasing expertise in CCTV monitoring, emergency response, and team supervision."

**For Senior:**
"Security Supervisor with 10+ years of experience managing 24/7 security operations for high-rise commercial complexes and critical infrastructure. Led team of 20 security officers across 3 shifts, achieving a 95% reduction in security incidents over 4 years. Developed comprehensive emergency response protocols adopted across 5 facility locations."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

### Core Security Skills
- Surveillance and CCTV monitoring
- Access control and badge verification
- Patrol operations (foot, vehicle, bicycle)
- Incident reporting and documentation
- Emergency response and evacuation procedures

### Technical Skills for Your Resume Template
- Security camera systems (Axis, Hikvision, Genetec)
- Access control systems (Lenel, S2, Honeywell)
- Alarm monitoring platforms
- Two-way radio communication
- Microsoft Office for reports and logs

### Certifications
- State security guard license (specify your state)
- CPR/First Aid/AED certification
- OSHA safety training
- Armed security license (if applicable)
- Defensive tactics or de-escalation training

## Security Guard Resume Format & Template Tips

Choosing the right resume format is critical for security and law enforcement roles:
- **Chronological resume format** — Best for security guard candidates with steady work history, listing your most recent security positions first to demonstrate experience growth
- **Professional resume format** — Use clean section headers, consistent spacing, and a modern but readable font like Calibri or Arial. Place your security license and certifications near the top
- **ATS-friendly resume template** — Avoid graphics, tables, and columns that confuse applicant tracking systems. Stick to standard section headers like "Experience," "Certifications," and "Skills"
- **Resume length** — 1 page for entry-level security guards, extending to 1-2 pages for supervisors and specialized security professionals
- Save your resume as PDF to preserve your resume format across all devices and ATS platforms
- Include your state security license number and expiration date prominently in the header or certifications section

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Missing license information** — Your state security guard license should be one of the first things visible on your resume
2. **No incident metrics** — Quantify patrol counts, incident resolutions, and facility sizes to prove your track record
3. **Ignoring technology skills** — CCTV systems, access control platforms, and alarm monitoring are essential modern security skills
4. **Vague duty descriptions** — Replace "patrolled the building" with "conducted 6 scheduled patrol rounds per 8-hour shift across a 500,000 sq. ft. facility"
5. **Overlooking de-escalation skills** — Conflict resolution and communication abilities are highly valued by employers

## ATS Optimization for Security Guard Resumes

Security companies and corporate HR departments use applicant tracking systems to screen candidates. To pass ATS screening with an ats resume format:
- Include keywords like "surveillance," "access control," "incident reporting," "patrol operations," "CCTV," and "emergency response" directly from the job posting
- Use an ats resume template with standard section headers — avoid creative layouts
- Spell out abbreviations at least once (e.g., "Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV)")
- List all licenses and certifications in a dedicated section with full names and license numbers
- Include facility types and sizes (e.g., "corporate campus," "retail center," "hospital," "750,000 sq. ft.")
- Mirror the exact language from the job description to maximize your ATS match score
