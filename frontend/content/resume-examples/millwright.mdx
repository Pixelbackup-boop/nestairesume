## What Makes a Great Millwright Resume?

Millwrights install, maintain, and repair industrial machinery. The trade requires precision—misalignment by thousandths of an inch can cause premature equipment failure. With +5% job growth and an average salary of $58,000, the Millwright field offers solid career opportunities for skilled tradespeople. Your resume must communicate your certifications, hands-on experience, and ability to work safely and efficiently. This guide covers what employers look for when hiring Millwright professionals.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Millwright:**
"Motivated Millwright with hands-on training in Machine Installation and Precision Alignment. Completed apprenticeship/training program with strong foundation in Rigging. Eager to apply skills in a professional environment with commitment to safety and quality work."

**For Experienced Millwright:**
"Skilled Millwright with 5+ years of experience in Machine Installation and Precision Alignment. Proven track record of completing projects on time while maintaining excellent safety record. Experienced in Rigging with strong troubleshooting abilities."

**For Senior Millwright:**
"Master Millwright with 15+ years of experience leading crews and managing complex projects. Expert in Machine Installation, Precision Alignment, and Rigging. Trained 20+ apprentices and maintained zero-incident safety record over past 5 years."


## Salary & Job Outlook

Millwright professionals earn a median annual salary of approximately **$58,000**, with experienced professionals and those with specialized certifications earning significantly more. Employment for this occupation is projected to grow **+5%** over the next decade.

## Millwright Resume Format & Template Tips

Trade resumes need to communicate competence quickly. Hiring managers often review dozens of applications—make yours stand out:

- **Certifications at the top** — License numbers, certification types, and expiration dates should be immediately visible. This is the first thing employers verify
- **Quantify your experience** — Years in trade, number of projects, production metrics, and safety record with specific numbers
- **Specify equipment and systems** — List specific brands, models, or systems you're experienced with. Generic descriptions don't differentiate you
- **Include safety record** — Incident-free years, safety training completed, and OSHA certifications demonstrate professionalism
- **Show progression** — Apprentice to journeyman to master, crew lead to foreman—show career growth
- **One page preferred** — Trades value efficiency. Keep it concise and relevant

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not specifying industry experience

Paper, power, mining, manufacturing—industry experience matters. Be specific about your background

### Missing precision alignment certifications

Laser alignment certification shows precision capability. Include all alignment training

### Ignoring rigging experience

Include maximum tonnage rigged and rigging certifications

### No shutdown/turnaround experience mentioned

Plant shutdowns require working under pressure. Include outage experience

### Not listing specific equipment types

Turbines, conveyors, pumps—list specific machinery you've installed and maintained



## Hiring Manager Tip

> **Millwright resumes that show certifications, productivity, and safety record get prioritized.**

Precision alignment is the millwright's calling card. "Journeyman millwright with 15 years experience in paper mill and power generation, laser alignment certified, rigging up to 100 tons." Include specific industries (paper, power, mining, manufacturing), tonnage rigged, and precision capabilities. Shutdown experience—working tight turnaround schedules—is valuable.


## Common Millwright Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Millwright interviews, along with guidance on how to answer them:

### "What industries have you worked in as a millwright?"

Discuss paper, power, manufacturing, or other industries and specific equipment.

### "Describe your precision alignment experience"

Cover laser alignment, dial indicators, and tolerance capabilities.

### "What is the largest equipment you've rigged?"

Discuss tonnage, rigging plans, and complex lifts you've performed.

### "Describe your experience with plant shutdowns"

Discuss working under time pressure, coordination, and critical path work.

### "What troubleshooting approach do you use for vibration issues?"

Cover vibration analysis, balance, alignment checks, and root cause identification.
