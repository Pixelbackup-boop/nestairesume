## What Makes a Great Roofer Resume?

Roofers protect buildings from the elements. The work is physically demanding, weather-dependent, and requires attention to detail—one missed nail can cause a leak. With +2% job growth and an average salary of $47,000, the Roofer field offers solid career opportunities for skilled tradespeople. Your resume must communicate your certifications, hands-on experience, and ability to work safely and efficiently. This guide covers what employers look for when hiring Roofer professionals.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Roofer:**
"Motivated Roofer with hands-on training in Shingle Installation and Flat Roofing. Completed apprenticeship/training program with strong foundation in Repairs. Eager to apply skills in a professional environment with commitment to safety and quality work."

**For Experienced Roofer:**
"Skilled Roofer with 5+ years of experience in Shingle Installation and Flat Roofing. Proven track record of completing projects on time while maintaining excellent safety record. Experienced in Repairs with strong troubleshooting abilities."

**For Senior Roofer:**
"Master Roofer with 15+ years of experience leading crews and managing complex projects. Expert in Shingle Installation, Flat Roofing, and Repairs. Trained 20+ apprentices and maintained zero-incident safety record over past 5 years."

## Roofer Resume Format & Template Tips

Trade resumes need to communicate competence quickly. Hiring managers often review dozens of applications—make yours stand out:

- **Certifications at the top** — License numbers, certification types, and expiration dates should be immediately visible. This is the first thing employers verify
- **Quantify your experience** — Years in trade, number of projects, production metrics, and safety record with specific numbers
- **Specify equipment and systems** — List specific brands, models, or systems you're experienced with. Generic descriptions don't differentiate you
- **Include safety record** — Incident-free years, safety training completed, and OSHA certifications demonstrate professionalism
- **Show progression** — Apprentice to journeyman to master, crew lead to foreman—show career growth
- **One page preferred** — Trades value efficiency. Keep it concise and relevant

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not specifying roofing system experience

Shingles, flat roof, metal, tile—be specific about your experience with each system

### Missing production metrics

Squares per day, roofs completed annually, crew productivity—numbers demonstrate capability

### Ignoring manufacturer certifications

GAF, CertainTeed, Owens Corning certifications show professionalism and warranty capability

### No mention of quality/callback rate

Low callback rates demonstrate quality installation. Include quality metrics if strong

### Not addressing safety experience

Roofing is dangerous. OSHA training and fall protection experience should be highlighted

## Common Roofer Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Roofer interviews, along with guidance on how to answer them:

### "What roofing systems are you most experienced with?"

Discuss shingles, flat roofing, metal, tile—your experience and certifications with each.

### "How do you ensure quality installations?"

Cover inspection processes, following manufacturer specs, and attention to detail points.

### "Describe your experience leading a roofing crew"

Discuss crew sizes, task delegation, quality control, and production management.

### "How do you handle safety on steep roofs?"

Cover fall protection, roof jacks, harnesses, and safety protocols. This is critical.

### "How do you assess and repair storm damage?"

Discuss damage identification, insurance documentation, and repair procedures.
