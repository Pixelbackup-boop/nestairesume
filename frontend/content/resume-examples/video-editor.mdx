## What Makes a Great Video Editor Resume?

A video editor resume must lead with visual proof of your abilities—your portfolio does the heavy lifting. But the resume itself needs to demonstrate you can deliver results, meet deadlines, and work professionally with clients and teams.

The most effective video editor resumes combine strong portfolio links with quantifiable impact: view counts, revenue attribution, client lists, and business outcomes that prove your creative work delivers value.

## Professional Summary Examples

### Entry-Level Video Editor
Video editor with film school training and YouTube channel experience. Proficient in Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve. Edited 100+ videos including short films, social content, and corporate work.

### Mid-Level Video Editor
Video Editor with 4 years cutting content for broadcast, digital, and social platforms. Edited work viewed 20M+ times. Expert in narrative pacing, color grading, and sound design. Specialize in documentary and branded content.

### Senior Video Editor
Senior Editor with 8+ years and credits on Emmy-nominated series and viral campaigns. Lead editor for production company handling $2M+ annual projects. Mentor junior editors and manage post-production workflows.

## Video Editor Resume Format & Template Tips

1. **Include reel link**
2. **Lead with view counts or reach metrics**
3. **List software proficiency with specific versions**
4. **Mention turnaround speed for time-sensitive content**
5. **Highlight any broadcast, film, or streaming credits**

## Hiring Manager Tip

> **"Your reel should show range and pacing sense. I also need editors who can work fast—include turnaround times or mention experience with tight deadlines."**

Production companies hire editors who combine creative storytelling with technical efficiency. Your reel should demonstrate range (narrative, commercial, social) and strong pacing instincts. Include view counts and any notable placements (broadcast, streaming). Fast turnaround capability is increasingly important for digital content.

## Common Video Editor Interview Questions

### 1. Walk me through your editing process
Describe assembly, rough cut, fine cut, color, and sound stages.

### 2. How do you approach editing for different platforms?
Discuss pacing, aspect ratios, and platform-specific considerations.

### 3. Tell me about your color grading workflow
Describe software, LUTs, color correction, and creative grading.

### 4. How do you handle a project with poor source material?
Show problem-solving with audio issues, coverage gaps, or quality problems.

### 5. Whats your approach to staying organized on large projects?
Discuss folder structure, naming conventions, and asset management.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **No reel or samples inaccessible**
2. **Reel too long (over 2 minutes) or poorly paced**
3. **Only one content type without showing range**
4. **Not mentioning software and technical capabilities**
5. **Generic descriptions without view counts or metrics**

## ATS Optimization for Video Editor Resumes

Many creative roles still use ATS systems for initial screening. Include these keywords naturally:

**Priority Keywords:** video editor, Premiere Pro, Final Cut, DaVinci Resolve, color grading

**Additional Keywords:** post-production, video editing, narrative editing, broadcast

While portfolio quality matters most, matching job posting language improves your chances of reaching human review.
