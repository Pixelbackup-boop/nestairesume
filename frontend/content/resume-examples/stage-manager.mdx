## What Makes a Great Stage Manager Resume?

This stage manager resume example shows exactly what hiring managers want to see. The right resume format makes the difference between landing an interview and getting filtered out. Stage managers are the organizational backbone of any production, and your resume must reflect exceptional coordination skills, calm under pressure, and attention to detail. Include your production credits, the scale of shows you have managed, and specific examples of how you kept complex productions running smoothly. Union membership (AEA/IATSE) and notable venue or company experience carry significant weight.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"Theatre graduate with stage management experience on 8 university and community theatre productions, including a 200-seat mainstage musical with a cast of 35. Skilled in cue calling, rehearsal scheduling, and production documentation using Stage Write and Google Suite. AEA Membership Candidate."

**For Mid-Level:**
"Stage Manager with 5 years of professional experience managing Equity and non-Equity productions across regional theatre, touring shows, and corporate events. Managed productions with budgets up to $150K, casts of 25+, and technical crews of 15. Known for meticulous prompt books and calm, clear cue calling."

**For Senior:**
"AEA Production Stage Manager with 10+ years of experience on Broadway national tours, Off-Broadway, and LORT theatres. Managed the national tour of a Tony-nominated musical across 42 cities over 18 months. Expert in large-scale production coordination, union protocols, and multi-departmental communication."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

### Production Management
- Cue calling and show running
- Prompt book creation and maintenance
- Rehearsal scheduling and management
- Production meeting coordination
- Script analysis and blocking notation
- Tech rehearsal and dress rehearsal oversight

### Technical Knowledge
- Lighting, sound, and scenic cue coordination
- Fly system and rigging awareness
- Stage Write, StagePro, and QLab familiarity
- Backstage traffic management
- Quick-change coordination
- Emergency procedures and safety protocols

### Communication & Leadership
- Director and designer liaison
- Cast and crew communication
- Conflict resolution under pressure
- Union (AEA/IATSE) protocol compliance
- Production report writing
- Multi-departmental coordination

## Achievement-Focused Bullet Points

- "Called 150+ performances of a regional theatre musical with 280 lighting cues, 95 sound cues, and 40 fly cues, maintaining a 99.5% accuracy rate"
- "Managed rehearsal schedules for 3 simultaneous productions at a LORT theatre, coordinating 45 actors and 20 crew members across overlapping timelines"
- "Oversaw the technical load-in for a touring production in 42 venues across 18 months, adapting to different stage configurations while maintaining show quality"
- "Created a comprehensive prompt book and production documentation system adopted as the standard template for the company's future productions"
- "Coordinated a 28-person quick-change team for a musical with 150+ costume changes, achieving seamless transitions with zero missed cues across a 6-week run"
- "Resolved a critical scenic automation malfunction during a live performance by implementing a manual backup plan within 30 seconds, with no audience disruption"

## Stage Manager Resume Format & Template Tips

Choosing the right resume format is critical for entertainment roles:
- **Chronological resume format** — Best for stage manager candidates with steady career progression from assistant stage manager to PSM
- **Professional resume format** — Use clean section headers, consistent spacing, and a modern but readable font
- **ATS-friendly resume template** — Avoid graphics, tables, and columns that confuse applicant tracking systems
- **Resume length** — 1 page for entry-level / 1-2 pages for experienced / 2 pages for senior
- Save your resume as PDF to preserve your resume format across all devices and ATS platforms

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Not listing specific production credits** — Include show titles, venues, directors, and your role (SM, ASM, PSM) in a clear credits section
2. **Omitting union status** — AEA membership or candidacy is critical information for professional theatre positions
3. **Being vague about scale** — Always specify cast size, crew size, number of cues, and venue capacity
4. **Ignoring non-theatre experience** — Corporate events, concerts, and film production work all demonstrate transferable skills
5. **Forgetting software skills** — Production management tools like Stage Write, QLab, and scheduling software should be included
