## What Makes a Great Chef Resume?

This chef resume example shows exactly what hiring managers and restaurant owners want to see. The right resume format makes the difference between landing interviews and getting filtered out. A standout chef resume goes beyond listing dishes — it demonstrates your leadership in the kitchen, your ability to control food costs, and the creativity you bring to menu development. Employers want to see measurable results alongside culinary passion.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level:**
"Culinary arts graduate with 2 years of line cook experience in high-volume restaurants serving 300+ covers nightly. Trained in classical French technique, modern plating, and food safety (ServSafe certified). Developed 5 seasonal specials that became permanent menu items. A professional resume reflecting a strong foundation and culinary creativity."

**For Mid-Level:**
"Sous Chef with 6 years of experience in fine dining and farm-to-table restaurants. Managed kitchen team of 10, maintained food cost at 28% consistently, and developed seasonal menus that increased repeat customers by 20%. Professional resume showcasing expertise in menu engineering, inventory management, and high-pressure kitchen operations."

**For Senior:**
"Executive Chef with 12+ years leading culinary operations for multi-unit restaurant groups generating $5M+ in annual revenue. Opened 3 restaurant concepts from scratch, each earning local 'Best New Restaurant' recognition. Reduced food waste by 35% while maintaining Michelin-quality standards and training 50+ culinary professionals."

## Essential Skills to Highlight

### Culinary Expertise
- Menu development and engineering
- Classical and modern cooking techniques
- Plating, garnishing, and food presentation
- Dietary accommodations (vegan, gluten-free, allergen management)
- Wine and beverage pairing

### Kitchen Management for Your Resume Template
- Kitchen staff supervision and scheduling
- Food cost control and P&L management
- Inventory management and ordering
- Health inspection and food safety compliance
- Vendor sourcing and relationship management

### Certifications
- ServSafe Food Protection Manager
- Certified Executive Chef (CEC) — ACF
- Certified Sous Chef (CSC) — ACF
- HACCP certification
- Culinary degree or apprenticeship credentials

## Chef Resume Format & Template Tips

Choosing the right resume format is critical for hospitality and culinary roles:
- **Chronological resume format** — Best for chef candidates with steady career progression, listing your most recent kitchen experience first to show your culinary evolution
- **Professional resume format** — Use clean section headers, consistent spacing, and a modern but readable font like Calibri or Arial. Include cuisine specialties and restaurant types prominently
- **ATS-friendly resume template** — Avoid graphics, tables, and columns that confuse applicant tracking systems. Stick to standard section headers like "Experience," "Culinary Skills," and "Certifications"
- **Resume length** — 1 page for line cooks and junior chefs, extending to 1-2 pages for sous chefs and executive chefs with multi-concept experience
- Save your resume as PDF to preserve your resume format across all devices and ATS platforms
- List your cuisine specialties, restaurant volume (covers per night), and management scope near the top

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **No business metrics** — Include food cost percentages, revenue figures, and covers served to prove your business acumen
2. **Ignoring leadership experience** — Highlight team sizes, training accomplishments, and kitchen management responsibilities
3. **Missing food safety certifications** — ServSafe and HACCP are non-negotiable credentials for chef positions
4. **Vague menu descriptions** — Specify the type of cuisine, menu size, and any recognition or awards your food received
5. **Overlooking cost control** — Demonstrate how you managed food costs, reduced waste, and improved profitability

## ATS Optimization for Chef Resumes

Larger restaurant groups and hospitality companies use applicant tracking systems to screen culinary candidates. To pass ATS screening with an ats resume format:
- Include keywords like "menu development," "food cost control," "kitchen management," "ServSafe," "inventory management," and "staff training" directly from the job posting
- Use an ats resume template with standard section headers — avoid creative layouts that may not parse correctly
- Spell out abbreviations at least once (e.g., "American Culinary Federation (ACF)")
- List specific cuisine types and cooking techniques mentioned in the job description
- Include restaurant volume metrics (e.g., "500+ covers daily," "fine dining," "banquet service for 200+")
- Mirror the exact language from the job description to maximize your ATS match score
