## What Makes a Great Pest Control Technician Resume?

Pest control technicians protect homes and businesses from pests. The role combines technical knowledge of pest biology, safe chemical application, and customer service. With +6% job growth and an average salary of $38,000, the Pest Control Technician field offers solid career opportunities for skilled tradespeople. Your resume must communicate your certifications, hands-on experience, and ability to work safely and efficiently. This guide covers what employers look for when hiring Pest Control Technician professionals.

## Professional Summary Examples

**For Entry-Level Pest Control Technician:**

"Skilled Pest Control Technician with 5+ years of experience in Pest Identification and Chemical Application. Proven track record of completing projects on time while maintaining excellent safety record. Experienced in IPM with strong troubleshooting abilities."

**For Senior Pest Control Technician:**
"Master Pest Control Technician with 15+ years of experience leading crews and managing complex projects. Expert in Pest Identification, Chemical Application, and IPM. Trained 20+ apprentices and maintained zero-incident safety record over past 5 years."

## Pest Control Technician Resume Format & Template Tips

Trade resumes need to communicate competence quickly. Hiring managers often review dozens of applications—make yours stand out:

- **Certifications at the top** — License numbers, certification types, and expiration dates should be immediately visible. This is the first thing employers verify
- **Quantify your experience** — Years in trade, number of projects, production metrics, and safety record with specific numbers
- **Specify equipment and systems** — List specific brands, models, or systems you're experienced with. Generic descriptions don't differentiate you
- **Include safety record** — Incident-free years, safety training completed, and OSHA certifications demonstrate professionalism
- **Show progression** — Apprentice to journeyman to master, crew lead to foreman—show career growth
- **One page preferred** — Trades value efficiency. Keep it concise and relevant

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Not listing specific certifications

General pest, termite, fumigation, wildlife—list all licenses and certifications held

### Missing route and retention metrics

Stops per day, accounts managed, retention rate—these show productivity and customer satisfaction

### Ignoring sales experience

Upselling services is often part of the job. Include sales achievements if applicable

### No IPM knowledge mentioned

Integrated Pest Management is industry standard. Show you understand more than just chemical application

### Not specifying pest types

General pest, termite, rodent, wildlife, bed bugs—specify your experience areas

## Common Pest Control Technician Interview Questions

Preparing for interviews is an important part of the job search process. Here are questions frequently asked in Pest Control Technician interviews, along with guidance on how to answer them:

### "What pest control licenses do you hold?"

List state licenses and any additional certifications like termite or wildlife.

### "Describe your experience with termite treatments"

Discuss liquid treatment, baiting systems, inspection procedures, and WDI reports.

### "How do you handle customer complaints about recurring pests?"

Discuss investigation, treatment adjustment, and communication.

### "What is your approach to route management?"

Discuss efficiency, scheduling, and maximizing stops while maintaining quality.

### "How do you explain treatment options to customers?"

Show you can communicate technical information clearly and build trust.
