Waiting to hear back after a job interview is one of the most stressful parts of the job search. But following up incorrectly can damage a candidacy that was going well. The right approach is professional, brief, and timed correctly.

Here is exactly how to follow up — and when — with templates for every scenario.

## How to Follow Up After an Interview: The Timeline

Understanding the expected cadence prevents you from following up too early (annoying) or too late (missing the window):

| Timing | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Within 24 hours of interview | Send thank you note |
| After stated timeline (or 5-7 business days if no timeline given) | First follow-up email |
| 5-7 business days after first follow-up (if no response) | Second follow-up email |
| After second follow-up with no response | Move the opportunity to "low priority" — continue applying elsewhere |

## Before You Follow Up: What You Should Know

### Check What Timeline They Gave You

At the end of any interview, the interviewer typically says something like:
- "We expect to make a decision by [date]"
- "We are moving quickly and hope to wrap up interviews this week"
- "We are still in early-stage interviews and it will be a few weeks"

Honor whatever timeline they gave you. If they said "two weeks" and it has been one week, wait. Following up before their stated timeline signals impatience.

### Confirm You Sent the Thank You Note

Your first follow-up should reference the thank you note you sent. If you did not send one, send the follow-up as a combined thank you + status check.

### Find the Right Person to Email

Email the person who scheduled your interview or the hiring manager directly. If you only have the recruiting coordinator's email, use that. If you have multiple contacts, start with whoever was most senior or whoever said they would be in touch.

## Follow-Up Email Templates

### Template 1: After the Stated Timeline Has Passed

**Subject:** Following Up — [Job Title] Interview on [Date]

Hi [Name],

I hope you are doing well. You mentioned the team expected to make a decision around [stated date], and I wanted to follow up to see if there are any updates on the [Job Title] position.

I remain very interested in the role and would welcome the opportunity to move forward. Please let me know if there is anything additional I can provide.

Best,
[Your Name]

---

### Template 2: No Timeline Given (5-7 Business Days After Thank You)

**Subject:** Checking In — [Job Title] Role

Hi [Name],

I wanted to follow up on the [Job Title] position and reiterate my strong interest in the role. I enjoyed our conversation on [date] and have been thinking more about [specific topic from interview].

Is there any update on your timeline for next steps? Please let me know if there is anything else you need from me.

Best,
[Your Name]

---

### Template 3: After Final Round With No Response

**Subject:** Final Round Follow-Up — [Job Title]

Hi [Name],

I hope all is well. I wanted to reach out following the final-round interviews last week. The conversations reinforced my enthusiasm for this role and the team — particularly [brief specific reference].

I understand that hiring decisions take time, and I want to respect your process. If there is any additional information that would be helpful as you finalize your decision, I am happy to provide it.

I look forward to hearing from you when you have an update.

Best,
[Your Name]

---

### Template 4: When You Have a Competing Offer

This is one of the most effective follow-up emails because it creates genuine urgency:

**Subject:** Update on My Application — [Job Title]

Hi [Name],

I wanted to provide a quick update: I have received an offer from another company with a decision deadline of [date].

[Company] remains my first choice, and I wanted to give you the opportunity to discuss next steps before I need to respond to the competing offer. Is there any possibility of an expedited timeline?

Thank you for your consideration.

Best,
[Your Name]

---

### Template 5: Second Follow-Up (If No Response to First)

**Subject:** Re: [Previous subject line]

Hi [Name],

I wanted to follow up once more on the [Job Title] position. I understand you may be busy, and I apologize if this is reaching you at a difficult time.

If the position is still open, I remain interested and available. If circumstances have changed or the role has been filled, I completely understand — I would simply appreciate a brief update when convenient.

Thank you,
[Your Name]

---

### Template 6: After You Have Sent Two Follow-Ups With No Response

At this point, your follow-up options are exhausted. Send a gracious final note:

**Subject:** Re: [Previous subject line]

Hi [Name],

I understand you must be very busy. I will step back from following up at this point, but I remain interested in opportunities at [Company] and would welcome the chance to reconnect in the future.

Thank you for your time during the interview process.

Best,
[Your Name]

Then move on and continue applying elsewhere.

## What to Do When the Hiring Process Is Delayed

Hiring processes frequently take longer than expected. Common reasons:
- Budget approvals pending
- Internal candidate being considered
- Hiring freeze or reorg
- A key decision-maker was traveling or unavailable
- The role's requirements changed

When you receive a delay response ("we need a few more weeks"), respond graciously:

"Thank you for the update — I completely understand. I remain very interested and look forward to hearing from you when the time is right."

This keeps the door open without pressure. Continue actively applying to other positions during this window.

## How to Stay Top of Mind Without Following Up Too Often

If you are in a prolonged waiting period (2-4 weeks with minimal communication), you can stay relevant without directly asking for an update:

**Share a relevant article or insight:**
"Hi [Name], I came across this article about [relevant industry topic] and thought of our conversation about [specific topic discussed]. Sharing in case it is useful."

This is a soft touchpoint that demonstrates continued engagement and intelligence without creating pressure.

**Connect on LinkedIn:**
If you are not already connected, send a connection request. This keeps your name visible in their network without requiring a direct follow-up email.

## Signs the Process Is Moving Forward (Without Explicit Communication)

Watch for indirect signals:
- They request references
- HR sends paperwork or background check requests
- You receive a calendar invite for a follow-up call
- A recruiter connects with you on LinkedIn

These are positive signals — do not follow up aggressively while these are in motion.

## Signs the Process May Be Over

- More than 4 weeks since final round with no communication after two follow-ups
- LinkedIn shows the role was reposted (may mean they rejected the candidate pool)
- The job listing was removed (may mean it was filled)
- A recruiter stops responding

At this point, accept the implicit rejection and redirect your energy. The process was likely genuine — hiring decisions involve internal factors you cannot see from the outside.

## How to Prepare Effectively

Effective interview preparation goes beyond memorizing answers. Start by researching the company thoroughly — understand their products, recent news, competitors, and company culture. Review the job description line by line and prepare specific examples from your experience that demonstrate each required qualification.

Practice the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioral questions. Prepare at least 5-7 stories from your career that showcase different competencies like leadership, problem-solving, teamwork, and resilience under pressure. Time yourself — most answers should be 60-90 seconds.

Prepare thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer. Questions about team dynamics, growth opportunities, and current challenges show genuine interest. Avoid asking about salary or benefits in the first round unless the interviewer brings it up.

On the day of the interview, arrive 10-15 minutes early. Bring multiple copies of your resume, a notepad, and a pen. Dress one level above the company's typical dress code. After the interview, send a personalized thank-you email within 24 hours referencing specific points from your conversation.
