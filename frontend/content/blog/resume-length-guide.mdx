"Keep it to one page." That's the most common resume advice — and it's only half right. A one-page resume is ideal for many job seekers, but forcing 15 years of senior experience onto a single page can actually hurt your chances.

The real answer depends on your experience level, industry, and the role you're targeting. Here's how to determine the right length for your situation.

## Ideal Resume Length: The Quick Answer

| Experience Level | Recommended Length |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Student / Entry-level (0-2 years) | **1 page** |
| Early career (3-5 years) | **1 page** |
| Mid-career (6-10 years) | **1 page** (2 if needed) |
| Senior (10-15 years) | **1-2 pages** |
| Executive / C-suite (15+ years) | **2 pages** |
| Academic / Federal | **2+ pages** (CV format) |

## When to Use a 1-Page Resume

A one-page resume works best when: A strong resume length — a topic that [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/) guidelines address for different career stages demonstrates this effectively.

- **You have less than 10 years of experience** — You likely don't have enough substantive content for two pages
- **You're applying for entry-level or junior roles** — Hiring managers expect concise resumes from early-career candidates
- **You're in a fast-paced industry** — Startups, tech, and creative industries value brevity
- **Most of your experience is in one field** — No need to document multiple career paths
- **The job posting is for an individual contributor role** — Not a leadership or management position

### How to fit everything on 1 page:

1. **Remove the objective/summary** if it's generic (keep it if it's strong)
2. **Cut jobs older than 10-15 years** unless they're directly relevant
3. **Use 3-4 bullet points per role** instead of 5-6
4. **Remove "References available upon request"** — it wastes a line and is assumed
5. **Consolidate skills** into a compact section (2-3 lines)
6. **Adjust margins** to 0.5-0.7 inches (don't go below 0.5)
7. **Use 10-11pt font** for body text (never below 10pt)
8. **Remove irrelevant experience** — a barista job from 2015 doesn't help your software engineering application

## When to Use a 2-Page Resume

A two-page resume is appropriate when:

- **You have 10+ years of relevant experience** and each role adds meaningful context
- **You're applying for senior, director, or executive positions** where breadth of experience matters
- **You have extensive technical projects** that demonstrate qualifications (engineering, IT, data science)
- **You're changing careers** and need to document transferable experience from a different field
- **You hold multiple certifications** relevant to the role
- **You're applying to federal government positions** which expect comprehensive documentation

### Rules for 2-page resumes:

1. **The most important content goes on page 1** — Many recruiters only read the first page in detail
2. **Page 2 must be at least half full** — A 1.5-page resume with empty space looks worse than 1 or 2 pages
3. **Include your name on page 2** — Add a header: "Jane Smith — Page 2" in case pages separate
4. **Don't pad with filler** — Every line should earn its place
5. **Most recent experience gets the most space** — 4-5 bullets for recent roles, 2-3 for older ones

## The 1.5-Page Problem

A resume that's 1.5 pages is the worst possible length. It signals one of two things to a recruiter:

1. You couldn't edit effectively (you have too little for 2 pages but couldn't cut to 1)
2. You padded content that should fit on 1 page

**If you're at 1.5 pages, choose a direction:**

### Option A: Cut to 1 page
- Remove the oldest job
- Reduce each role to 3 bullet points
- Tighten your skills section
- Remove any lines that describe duties rather than achievements

### Option B: Expand to 2 full pages
- Add a "Key Projects" section highlighting 2-3 significant projects
- Expand bullet points on recent roles with additional metrics
- Add a "Professional Development" section with certifications and courses
- Include relevant volunteer work or board memberships

## Industry-Specific Guidelines

### Technology
**Preferred length:** 1 page (early/mid), 2 pages (senior/staff/principal)

Tech recruiters scan quickly. They want to see: your tech stack, company names, and achievements. Senior engineers with 10+ years and multiple significant projects can justify 2 pages. Include a "Technical Skills" section that lists languages, frameworks, and tools.

### Finance & Consulting
**Preferred length:** 1 page (analyst/associate), 2 pages (VP and above)

Finance is one of the most page-count-conscious industries. Analysts and associates should always use 1 page. Managing directors and partners can use 2. Investment banking resumes are almost always 1 page regardless of experience.

### Healthcare
**Preferred length:** 1-2 pages depending on credentials

Nurses, therapists, and clinical staff typically need 1-2 pages to list certifications, licenses, and clinical rotations. Physicians use CVs (which have no page limit). Always include license numbers and certification dates.

### Education
**Preferred length:** 1-2 pages (teachers), CV for professors

K-12 teachers typically use 1-2 pages. Include teaching certifications, endorsements, and measurable student outcomes. University professors and researchers use academic CVs that can run 5-20+ pages.

### Federal Government
**Preferred length:** 2-5 pages (USAJobs format)

Federal resumes follow a different standard entirely. They're longer, more detailed, and include information like salary history, supervisor names, and hours worked per week. Follow the USAJobs format exactly.

### Creative & Design
**Preferred length:** 1 page + portfolio link

Designers, writers, and creatives should keep the resume to 1 page and direct recruiters to an online portfolio. Your work samples speak louder than additional resume pages.

## Formatting for Each Length

### 1-page resume formatting:
```
Margins: 0.5-0.75 inches
Font size: 10-11pt body, 12-14pt headers
Sections: Summary, Experience (3-4 roles), Skills, Education
Bullet points: 3-4 per role
```

### 2-page resume formatting:
```
Margins: 0.75-1.0 inches
Font size: 10.5-11pt body, 12-14pt headers
Sections: Summary, Experience (5-7 roles), Skills, Education,
          Certifications, Projects, Awards
Bullet points: 4-5 for recent roles, 2-3 for older roles
Page 2 header: Name — Page 2
```

## How Recruiters Actually Read Resumes: The 7-Second Scan

The often-cited statistic is that recruiters spend **7.4 seconds** on an initial resume scan. But what does that scan actually look like? Eye-tracking studies from Ladders Research reveal a consistent pattern that directly affects how you should think about resume length and layout.

### What Recruiters Look at First (In Order)

1. **Your name and current job title** (0-1 seconds) -- They're establishing who you are and what level you're at. If your title doesn't match the role they're hiring for, attention drops immediately.

2. **Current company name** (1-2 seconds) -- Brand recognition matters. A recognizable company name (Google, Deloitte, Mayo Clinic) buys you more reading time. If the company isn't well-known, a brief descriptor helps: "Acme Corp (Series B fintech startup, 200 employees)."

3. **Current role dates** (2-3 seconds) -- They're checking tenure. Less than a year raises a flag. More than 2 years at your current role signals stability.

4. **Previous job title and company** (3-4 seconds) -- They're looking for career progression. Did you move up, laterally, or down? Consistent growth keeps them reading.

5. **Education** (4-5 seconds) -- For entry-level roles, this is weighted heavily. For senior roles, it's a quick confirmation and they move on.

6. **Skills section** (5-7 seconds) -- A quick keyword scan looking for specific technologies, certifications, or competencies that match the job description.

### What This Means for Resume Length

The 7-second scan happens on **page 1 only**. If your strongest qualifications are buried on page 2, they won't be seen during the initial screen. This is why page 1 layout matters more than total page count.

**For 1-page resumes:** Every section competes for attention. Put your strongest content (title, company, achievements) in the top third of the page.

**For 2-page resumes:** Treat page 1 as your highlight reel and page 2 as supporting evidence. Your professional summary, most recent role, and key skills must all appear on page 1. Page 2 can hold earlier career history, certifications, projects, and education details.

### The "F-Pattern" Reading Behavior

After the initial 7-second scan, recruiters who continue reading follow an F-pattern: they read the first line of each section fully, then scan down the left margin of subsequent lines. This means:

- **The first bullet point under each job is the most important** -- make it your strongest achievement
- **Bullet points that start with bold numbers or metrics catch the eye** as it scans down the left side
- **Long paragraphs get skipped entirely** -- always use bullet points, not narrative text
- **Section headers act as signposts** -- clear, standard headers (Experience, Skills, Education) let recruiters navigate quickly

Understanding the 7-second scan doesn't mean your resume should be shorter. It means **your most impressive content must be at the top of page 1**, regardless of whether your resume is one page or two.

## Common Resume Length Mistakes

Avoiding these mistakes will make your resume length stand out. 1. **Using a tiny font to fit one page** — If you're at 8pt font, you need two pages
2. **Removing margins to fit content** — Margins below 0.5" make your resume unreadable
3. **Listing every job you've ever had** — Focus on the last 10-15 years
4. **Including full addresses** — City and state (or just metro area) is sufficient
5. **Writing paragraphs instead of bullets** — Bullets are scannable; paragraphs aren't
6. **Padding page 2 with interests and hobbies** — Unless they're directly relevant, leave them off
