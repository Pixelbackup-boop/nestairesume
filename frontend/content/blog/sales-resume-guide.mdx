Sales hiring is numbers-driven, and sales resumes follow the same logic. Hiring managers want to know your quota, your attainment, your deal size, and your rank among peers — and they want those numbers visible on the first scan.

If your sales resume does not lead with specific revenue numbers and quota percentages, it will not compete with candidates who do.

## What Sales Hiring Managers Look For First

Every sales hiring manager evaluates the same core questions when reviewing a resume:

1. **What was your quota?** — Size matters. $200K quota vs. $1.5M quota indicates different market segments and deal complexity
2. **Did you hit it?** — Attainment percentage: below 80% raises concerns, 100%+ is table stakes, 120%+ is strong
3. **How consistently?** — One good year vs. 3 consecutive years of quota attainment tells very different stories
4. **What deal size did you work?** — Enterprise ($100K+ ACV) vs. mid-market ($15-80K) vs. SMB (under $15K)
5. **What type of sale?** — New logo vs. expansion/upsell, product vs. solution vs. services
6. **What tools and methodology?** — CRM proficiency, sales framework alignment

Answer all six questions on your resume and you have an excellent starting point.

## Sales Resume Format Tips

### 1. Lead Every Role With Quota Context
Open each role's description with: quota size, attainment percentage, and rank if strong. Example: "Quota: $1.2M | Attainment: 118% | Ranked 3 of 22 AEs." This immediately tells the reader everything they need to know.

### 2. Separate New Business From Expansion
If your role included both new logo and expansion/upsell, break them out. "Closed $2M in new logo ARR and managed $3M in expansion ARR" is more informative than "$5M in total ARR."

### 3. Show Consistency Over Time
One good year could be luck. Four good years is a track record. If you have consistent quota attainment, show multiple years explicitly: "Achieved 100%+ of quota for 4 consecutive fiscal years."

### 4. Use Sales-Specific Abbreviations Correctly
AEs, SDRs, ACV, ARR, MRR, NRR, CAC, LTV, QBR, MEDDIC, POC, POV — use these terms naturally. They signal insider knowledge and optimize for ATS keyword matching in SaaS and tech hiring.

### 5. List CRM Proficiency at the Right Depth
Not just "Salesforce" — "Salesforce (pipeline management, opportunity stages, custom reports, forecasting, Salesforce CPQ)" shows you know the tool at depth.

## Common Sales Resume Mistakes

**1. "Exceeded quota" without the quota or percentage**
This is the most common mistake. "Exceeded quota consistently" → "Achieved 116% of $900K annual quota; top 15% of 30-person sales team."

**2. Activity metrics without results**
"Made 80 calls per day" → "Made 75+ daily calls at 9% connection rate, generating 6 qualified demos per week."

**3. Missing the employer's market context**
"Sold enterprise SaaS" → "Sold enterprise HR tech (ATS, payroll, benefits) to HR directors and CHROs at companies with 500-5,000 employees."

**4. Not differentiating your deal type**
Direct sales vs. channel, product vs. services, transactional vs. consultative — be specific. These are not equivalent experiences.

## How ATS Systems Work in 2026

Applicant Tracking Systems have evolved significantly. Modern ATS platforms like Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, and iCIMS use advanced parsing algorithms that go beyond simple keyword matching. They analyze context, evaluate skill relevance, and even assess candidate-job fit using machine learning.

When you submit your resume, the ATS extracts text and organizes it into structured data fields: contact information, work experience, education, and skills. The system then scores your application against the job requirements. Resumes that score below a threshold may never reach human eyes.

To optimize for ATS success, use standard section headings that the system can recognize. Avoid headers and footers — many ATS platforms cannot read content placed there. Stick to common fonts and avoid text boxes, tables, or graphics that can confuse the parser. Submit in PDF or DOCX format as specified in the job posting.

Keyword optimization is essential but must feel natural. Mirror the exact phrasing from the job description — if they say "project management," do not substitute "managed projects." Include both acronyms and full terms (e.g., "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)"). But avoid keyword stuffing — modern ATS algorithms can detect and penalize this practice.
