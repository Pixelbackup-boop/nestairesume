The professional summary is your resume's headline. It sits at the top of the page, it is the first thing recruiters read, and it determines in roughly six seconds whether they keep reading or move on.

Most summaries fail because they are generic ("Highly motivated professional with excellent communication skills") or boring ("Seeking a challenging role where I can grow"). This guide shows you what strong summaries look like — with 20+ real examples across industries — and teaches you to write one that works.

## What Makes a Great Resume Summary

A strong professional summary does three things: A strong resume summary examples demonstrates this effectively.

1. **Identifies your professional identity** — who you are and at what level
2. **Highlights your most impressive credential or achievement** — the number or result that proves you can deliver
3. **Signals relevance** — why you are a fit for this type of role

It does NOT include:
- Vague adjectives ("dynamic," "results-driven," "passionate") without evidence
- "I" or "My" — summaries are written in third-person implied ("Experienced engineer with...")
- What you want ("Seeking a role where...") — focus on what you offer
- Information repeated verbatim from your first job bullet

## The Professional Summary Formula

```
[Job title identity] with [X years] of experience in [context/industry].
[Top achievement with metric]. [Key area of expertise] and [key area of expertise].
[One forward-looking sentence connecting your skills to the role — optional].
```

This is a template, not a rigid script. The best summaries vary sentence structure and feel natural. Use this as a starting framework, then write it in your own voice.

## How to Write Your Own Summary

### Step 1: Write It Last

Write your professional summary after you have completed all other resume sections. You cannot summarize what you have not yet described.

### Step 2: Pull Your Best Metric

Scan your work experience bullets and identify the single most impressive, specific achievement — the number that makes you look strongest. That goes in your summary.

### Step 3: Identify the Keywords

Review the job description for the role you are targeting. What 3-4 skills or experiences are mentioned most frequently? Those belong in your summary, in the employer's language.

### Step 4: Write Three Versions

Write a 2-sentence version, a 3-sentence version, and a 4-sentence version. Choose whichever fits best in your layout and feels most natural. Do not force length.

### Step 5: Remove Every Generic Phrase

Before submitting, search for: "highly motivated," "strong communicator," "team player," "passionate," "results-driven," "detail-oriented," "hard worker." Cut every one unless it is followed by concrete evidence. These phrases say nothing without proof.
