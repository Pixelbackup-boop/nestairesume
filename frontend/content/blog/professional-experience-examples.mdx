Your professional experience section is the most important part of your resume. Hiring managers spend **80% of their review time** on this section, and ATS systems extract keywords primarily from your work history. Yet most resumes describe job duties instead of professional achievements — and that's the difference between getting an interview and getting filtered out.

This guide provides real professional experience examples across industries, with the exact formulas to transform your experience section from a job description into an interview magnet.

## How to Write Professional Experience (The Formula)

The [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/) tracks employment projections across all industries, and one pattern is universal: employers hire candidates who demonstrate measurable impact. A [SHRM survey](https://www.shrm.org/) found that 75% of HR professionals use experience sections as the primary basis for interview decisions.

Every professional experience bullet point should follow this structure:

**Action Verb + What You Did + Measurable Result**

```
❌ Weak: "Responsible for managing the marketing budget"
✅ Strong: "Managed $2.4M annual marketing budget, reducing cost-per-lead
          by 31% through channel reallocation and programmatic optimization"
```

The difference is specificity and impact. The first describes a duty. The second proves competence with numbers.

### The STAR Framework for Bullet Points

- **Situation:** What was the context? (implied, not stated)
- **Task:** What were you responsible for?
- **Action:** What specifically did you do?
- **Result:** What was the measurable outcome?

You don't need all four in every bullet — but every bullet needs an **Action** and a **Result**.

## Professional Experience Examples by Industry

### Technology & Software

```
Software Engineer | TechCorp Inc. | 2022–Present
• Architected event-driven microservices system processing 2M+ daily
  transactions, reducing latency by 45% and infrastructure costs by $180K/year
• Led migration of monolithic application to Kubernetes, achieving 99.99%
  uptime and cutting deployment time from 4 hours to 12 minutes
• Mentored 4 junior engineers through code reviews and pair programming,
  reducing team onboarding time by 60%
```

**Why it works:** Names specific technologies, quantifies scale (2M transactions), and shows both technical and leadership impact.

### Marketing & Digital

```

  content strategy targeting 150+ high-intent keywords
```

**Why it works:** Every bullet has a dollar amount or percentage. Shows strategic thinking (keyword targeting, channel allocation) alongside execution.

## Professional Experience for Career Changers

If you're switching industries, reframe your experience using transferable language:

```
❌ Industry-specific: "Managed restaurant inventory using FIFO rotation"
✅ Transferable: "Managed $50K weekly inventory budget, reducing waste by
   20% through demand forecasting and optimized ordering systems"
```

**Key transferable skills to highlight:**
- Project management → "Led cross-functional initiative..."
- Budget management → "Managed $X budget..."
- Team leadership → "Supervised team of X..."
- Process improvement → "Streamlined workflow, reducing..."
- Client management → "Managed relationships with X accounts..."

## Professional Experience Section Formatting

### Standard format:
```
Job Title | Company Name | City, State | Start Date–End Date
• Achievement bullet with metric
• Achievement bullet with metric
• Achievement bullet with metric
```

### How many bullets per job?
- **Current/most recent role:** 4-6 bullets
- **Previous roles:** 3-4 bullets
- **Older roles (5+ years ago):** 2-3 bullets
- **Very old roles (10+ years):** 1-2 bullets or consolidate into "Additional Experience"

### Action verbs to start each bullet:
| Impact Level | Verbs |
|-------------|-------|
| **Leadership** | Led, Directed, Spearheaded, Oversaw, Managed |
| **Achievement** | Achieved, Exceeded, Delivered, Generated, Grew |
| **Improvement** | Optimized, Streamlined, Reduced, Improved, Transformed |
| **Creation** | Built, Developed, Designed, Launched, Established |
| **Analysis** | Analyzed, Evaluated, Assessed, Identified, Researched |

## Common Mistakes in Professional Experience Sections

### 1. Writing job descriptions instead of achievements
"Responsible for managing social media accounts" → "Grew Instagram following from 5K to 45K in 8 months, generating 200+ qualified leads per month through organic content strategy"

### 2. Missing metrics entirely
If you can't find exact numbers, estimate responsibly: "approximately," "~," or use ranges. "Managed team of 8-12" is better than "Managed team."

### 3. Including irrelevant experience
A senior software engineer doesn't need to list their college retail job. Curate for relevance, not completeness.

### 4. Using passive voice
"Sales targets were exceeded" → "Exceeded sales targets by 28%." Active voice is more compelling and takes up fewer words.

### 5. Inconsistent formatting
If your first bullet starts with a verb, every bullet should start with a verb. If dates are on the right for one job, they should be on the right for all jobs.

## ATS Tips for Your Experience Section

- Use the exact job title from the posting in your experience section if it's truthful
- Include industry-specific keywords naturally within bullet points
- Spell out acronyms on first use: "Customer Relationship Management (CRM)"
- Use standard section headers: "Professional Experience" or "Work Experience"
- Avoid tables, columns, or text boxes in the experience section
