A **simple resume template** is not a compromise — it is a strategic choice. In a landscape where applicant tracking systems reject over 75% of resumes before a human sees them, and recruiters spend an average of seven seconds on initial screening, clarity and structure beat decoration every time.

This guide explains why simple templates consistently outperform complex designs, how to choose and customize one for your industry, and where to find the best free options across Microsoft Word, Google Docs, and online resume builders like our [free builder](/en/builder).

## Why Simple Resume Templates Work

The case for simplicity is not about laziness or lack of design skill. It is about understanding how resumes are actually evaluated in 2026.

### ATS Systems Reward Structure

Applicant tracking systems parse resumes by identifying text patterns, section headings, and document structure. Simple templates with standard headings ("Work Experience," "Education," "Skills") parse correctly on every major ATS platform — Taleo, Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, and BambooHR.

Complex templates with tables, text boxes, multi-column layouts, and graphics confuse parsers. The result: your carefully crafted experience section gets misread, keywords are missed, and your application scores lower than it should. A simple template eliminates this risk entirely.

### Recruiters Scan, They Don't Read

Eye-tracking research consistently shows that recruiters follow an F-shaped reading pattern: they scan the left side of the page, read the top third more carefully, and quickly skim the rest. A simple, single-column layout aligns perfectly with this behavior. Every section falls exactly where the recruiter expects it.

Multi-column layouts, sidebars, and scattered information sections force recruiters to hunt for key details. When they are reviewing 200 resumes in an afternoon, the one that makes information easy to find wins.

### Content Stands on Its Own

When there is no visual noise — no icons, color blocks, progress bars, or decorative elements — your words have to do all the work. This is actually an advantage. A simple template forces you to write tighter bullet points, choose stronger action verbs, and quantify your achievements. The resume succeeds or fails on its content, which is exactly what it should be judged on.

## When to Choose Simple Over Creative

The default answer is almost always "simple." But here are the specific situations where a simple template is the unambiguously correct choice.

### Corporate and Enterprise Roles

Large companies run sophisticated ATS systems and expect professional, conservative presentations. Finance, accounting, consulting, operations, and general management roles at Fortune 500 companies almost universally prefer simple, clean resumes. If the company's website uses a minimalist design, your resume should match that sensibility.

### Legal and Compliance Positions

Law firms and compliance departments value precision and convention. A resume for a legal role should follow established formatting traditions: conservative fonts, clear chronological structure, and no visual embellishments. Partners reviewing associate applications expect this format.

### Healthcare and Medical Roles

Healthcare hiring prioritizes credentials, certifications, and clinical experience. A simple template that puts licenses and board certifications front and center — without burying them in a sidebar or infographic — serves nurses, physicians, pharmacists, and allied health professionals better than any creative design.

### Government and Public Sector

Federal, state, and municipal applications often have strict formatting requirements. Many government systems (including USAJOBS) strip formatting entirely and re-display your content in plain text. A simple template ensures nothing is lost in translation.

### When You Are Unsure About the Company Culture

If you cannot determine whether the company favors modern or traditional aesthetics, default to simple. A clean, well-structured resume never offends. A creative template aimed at the wrong audience can cost you the interview.

## Essential Sections of a Simple Resume

Every simple resume template should include these sections in this order (with minor adjustments based on experience level).

### Contact Information

Place your name at the top (14-16pt, bold), followed by phone number, professional email, city and state (full address is unnecessary), and LinkedIn URL. Keep this section to 2-3 lines. Do not use a header or footer for contact information — many ATS systems skip header/footer content.

### Professional Summary or Objective

A 2-3 sentence summary immediately below your contact information tells the recruiter who you are professionally. For experienced candidates, use a summary highlighting your specialization and top achievements. For entry-level candidates, a brief objective works. See our [professional summary writing guide](/en/blog/how-to-write-professional-summary) for detailed examples.

### Work Experience

List positions in reverse chronological order. For each role, include:

- **Job Title** — bold
- **Company Name** — followed by city, state
- **Dates of Employment** — month/year format, right-aligned or on a separate line
- **3-5 bullet points** — each starting with an action verb and including a measurable result

This is the section that determines whether you get the interview. Invest the most time here. Our [action verbs guide](/en/blog/resume-action-verbs) provides 200+ options organized by function.

### Education

Include degree, institution, graduation date, and relevant honors or GPA (if above 3.5 and within the last 5 years). For experienced professionals, education takes less space and moves below work experience. For students and recent graduates, it moves above.

### Skills

List 8-15 relevant skills, prioritized by relevance to the target role. Include both technical skills (software, tools, languages) and professional competencies (project management, data analysis, client relations). Match skills to keywords in the job description — this is where ATS keyword matching happens most directly.

### Optional Sections

Depending on your background, add certifications, publications, volunteer work, or professional affiliations. These sections strengthen your candidacy but should not crowd out the core sections above.

## Formatting a Simple Resume for Maximum Clarity

The details of formatting separate a good simple resume from a great one.

### Font Selection

| Font | Size | Best For |
|------|------|----------|
| Calibri | 11pt | Default on modern Word documents; clean and widely used |
| Arial | 10.5-11pt | Universal readability; safe for any industry |
| Helvetica | 10.5-11pt | Slightly more polished than Arial; popular in tech |
| Garamond | 11-12pt | Traditional elegance; ideal for legal and academic |
| Cambria | 11pt | Professional serif option; good for finance and consulting |

Stick to one font throughout the document. Use bold for headings and job titles, italic sparingly for company names or degrees.

### Margins and Spacing

- **Margins:** 0.5-1 inch on all sides (0.75 is the most common choice)
- **Line spacing:** 1.15-1.3 for body text
- **Section spacing:** 10-14pt between sections
- **Bullet point indentation:** 0.25 inches from the left margin

### Alignment and Consistency

Left-align all text. Right-align dates if you want a traditional look. Never center body text — it breaks the reader's eye flow. Most importantly, pick a formatting pattern and apply it consistently to every entry. If your first job listing puts the company name in bold, every job listing must do the same.

## Simple Resume Templates Across Platforms

### Microsoft Word

Word offers built-in resume templates via **File > New > search "resume."** The cleanest options are "Basic Resume" and "Simple Resume." However, many Word templates use tables, text boxes, and formatting elements that cause ATS parsing issues.

**Pros:** Full control over formatting; works offline; .docx is universally accepted.
**Cons:** Built-in templates often use ATS-unfriendly tables; formatting can shift between Word versions; no AI content assistance.

**Recommendation:** If using Word, start with a blank document and build your resume from scratch using simple formatting. Avoid the pre-built templates unless you verify they do not use tables or text boxes.

### Google Docs

Google Docs offers free templates via **docs.google.com/templates.** The "Coral," "Spearmint," and "Swiss" templates are the simplest options. Google Docs resumes can be downloaded as PDF or DOCX.

**Pros:** Free, cloud-based, easy to share, accessible from any device.
**Cons:** Limited design control compared to Word; some templates use columns that cause ATS issues; formatting can shift when downloading as DOCX.

**Recommendation:** Google Docs templates work well for quick, simple resumes. For the best ATS compatibility, download as PDF and test with an ATS checker. See our detailed guide on [creating resumes in Google Docs](/en/blog/resume-maker-google-docs).

## Customizing a Simple Template for Your Industry

### Finance and Accounting

Emphasize quantified achievements: revenue managed, cost savings, audit results, portfolio performance. Use conservative formatting — Garamond or Cambria, no accent colors, one page unless you have 15+ years of experience. Certifications (CPA, CFA, CFP) should be listed immediately after your name or in a dedicated section near the top.

### Information Technology

Lead with a technical skills section organized by category (Languages, Frameworks, Cloud/DevOps, Databases). Include GitHub, portfolio, or personal site URLs in your contact section. IT recruiters scan for specific technologies first, so make these easy to find.

### Legal

Use a chronological format with detailed descriptions of practice areas, case types, and bar admissions. Legal resumes are typically more text-dense than other industries — this is expected. Include publications, speaking engagements, and pro bono work.

### Healthcare

Place licenses, certifications, and clinical rotations prominently. Use standard medical terminology in your experience section. Healthcare recruiters filter by credentials first, then experience. A simple template that makes these instantly visible is essential.

### Education

List teaching experience, curriculum development, certifications (state teaching licenses), and professional development. Include student outcomes and assessment data where available. Education resumes often include a teaching philosophy statement — keep it to 2-3 sentences on the resume.

## Common Mistakes with Simple Resumes

Avoiding these mistakes will make your simple resume template stand out. ### Confusing "Simple" with "Bare"

A simple resume is streamlined, not empty. Every section should contain substantive content. If your resume looks sparse, you need stronger bullet points and more detail — not a fancier template.

### Using Default Word Formatting

Opening Word, typing your information, and hitting save does not produce a good simple resume. Even minimalist templates require intentional font choices, consistent heading styles, proper margins, and strategic use of bold and white space. Default formatting looks lazy, not simple.

### Ignoring Keywords

Simple templates work best when paired with [keyword-optimized content](/en/blog/resume-keywords-by-industry). Review the job description, identify 10-15 critical keywords, and integrate them naturally into your experience and skills sections. A clean template with poor keyword coverage still gets filtered out by ATS.

### Making It Too Long

Simplicity and conciseness go together. For most candidates, one page is the target. If your simple resume extends to two pages, ask whether every bullet point earns its space. Remove duties and keep accomplishments. Cut filler phrases like "Responsible for" and "Tasked with." For guidance on optimal length, see our [resume length guide](/en/blog/resume-length-guide).

### Not Tailoring for Each Application

A simple template makes tailoring easy — you are working with straightforward text, not wrestling with design elements. Take advantage of this. For each application, adjust your professional summary, reorder your skills, and emphasize the most relevant experience. A customized simple resume outperforms a generic creative one every time.
